## 1. Purpose: what a business is operating

The Secure Local Hub (SLH) is a **locally administered backend authority with a partially implemented Main Computer application**. It manages the resources of **one local organization**, enrolls workers and requesters, controls private computation, and can optionally participate in the Stable Hub/Ring 2 marketplace through logical public Worker sessions using SLH-controlled authorization and a verified organization-wallet binding. Individual physical Workers do not need marketplace wallets or marketplace signing keys. The MCEL SLH Overview and Worker Bank now exist as a synthesized, source-authored first slice; local first-run startup, P-256 bootstrap, basic Organization Service configuration, integrated administrator login and session revocation are now implemented in MCEL, but in-app Worker enrollment and full first-run acceptance on the user's Windows installation remain outstanding. The local control plane, separately enabled Organization Service, and Worker/marketplace pathways exist independently of that application.

The **Secure External Hub (SEH)** is **not** the SLH. It is a separately deployed, publicly reachable **untrusted relay**. Its name does not relax TLS or end-to-end authentication requirements. Canonical SEH deployment/inspection and outbound connection machinery exist through operator harnesses and local/Organization Service controls; **management from the future MCEL SLH application and complete remote-phone administration remain targets**. Coolify hosts the external relay, never the SLH identity or wallet authority. SEH may forward authorized opaque traffic, but cannot approve devices, spend credits, make access-policy decisions, or inspect private work.

The first enrollment of **any LMS-SLH identity** requires organization authorization, regardless of whether it is used by a person or a device. Source-backed Known Device keys and short-lived sessions are implemented; enrollment is neither a permanent login nor blanket administrator privilege. Organization policy may later require **a second independently held identity** (for example, on a phone) for step-up approval; it uses the **same identity cryptography**, not a separate human-identity class. That optional second-factor UI remains a target. Marketplace wallet signing authority is separate from local authentication.

### 1.1 Roles

| Role | Owns / can do | Cannot do by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SLH administrator | Configure local SLH, approve first enrollment, issue/revoke credentials, set policy, connect/deploy SEH, control public worker delegation | Automatically sign on behalf of every worker/user |
| Organization SLH backend | Known Device public-key registry, authentication policy, Worker scheduler, private-use journal and conditional public Worker proxy | Prove unreported private work occurred or mint external credits |
| Secure Worker | Execute assigned jobs; authenticate by device credential and operator policy | Possess SLH marketplace wallet keys by default |
| Requester | Authenticate, request private work, receive results | Enroll itself or modify SLH policy without admin authority |
| Phone Authenticator/Wallet | Approve operations, authenticate person, optionally sign wallet transactions | Implicitly become a worker or organization administrator |
| SEH public relay | Accept authorized network tunnels, rendezvous, forward opaque frames, expose minimal health | Mint identities, authorize business actions, decrypt work, settle credits |
| Public Ring 2 Hub | Marketplace admission, credits, authorization, work settlement | Access private SLH jobs or act as SLH identity authority |

## 2. Using SLH: from first launch to operating Workers

**Governing user journey:** The administrator starts with the **application**, not an already-configured Organization Service or a command-line login token. Opening SLH must work even on an unconfigured installation. The application discovers the local state, explains the next authorized step, and progressively makes capabilities available as their prerequisites become true. MCEL decides the presentation; the sequence below constrains behavior and authority, **not widgets, pages, or layout**.

The application has two separate operational planes. The **LMS-SLH (frontend/local-management SLH)** owns **all local identities under one model and API**, including the ordinary identity selected as this installation's default and identities used by local people or processes, with authorized key custody, discovery and signing. It also owns first bootstrap, protected local configuration and Organization Service lifecycle. **Default identity creation precedes but never implies organization enrollment or a special machine-only identity type.** The **Organization Service** is a separately enabled TLS process that authenticates Workers, requesters and administrators for organizational operations. A session issued by the first process **cannot** simply be reused in the second: sessions are process-local and expire. Requiring an Organization Service session before the Organization Service has been configured would create a circular prerequisite.

**Full-flow acceptance still outstanding:** An administrator can open the application with **no preconfigured Organization Service**, learn the real state, initialize and configure through authorized local steps, bring the Organization Service online, authenticate, enroll a Worker, and observe that Worker. The application may use a securely mediated host operation when local process startup is needed. It must **not** demand that the user first run `tools.mcel_slh_admin_session`, manually supply an Organization Service bearer, or perform undocumented setup outside the application. Those are temporary development fallbacks, not the intended product workflow.

**Current development boundary (November 9):** MCEL implements one-click first-administrator login for a truly uninitialized local organization, including the protected P-256 bootstrap and loopback-first service activation. The obsolete Admin ID, Admin Name, and P256 acknowledgment form is not displayed. Only existing LMS-SLH identity keys are used; personal TOTP is not implemented, and no unauthorized recovery shortcut is provided. Disposable-backend acceptance does not establish Windows production acceptance. The application does not yet enroll or launch Workers, enumerate network interfaces for selection, disable/restart the Organization Service, or provide phone/SEH configuration. `ORG_TRANSPORT_UNAVAILABLE` from the legacy CLI means an HTTPS exchange failed; it never by itself proves that service is unconfigured. The CLI session helper is not required for the new in-app login path.

### State distinctions the application must preserve

| Observation | Correct next action / explanation | Must not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local control probe cannot connect | Explain **local service unreachable**; offer authorized on-demand start or diagnostic guidance | “Not configured,” “no administrator,” “no Workers,” or “bad credentials” |
| Local service reachable; no default installation identity observed | LMS-SLH distinguishes a genuinely fresh installation from damaged or migrated identity state; **[TARGET]** create the new default locally or report recovery | Absence of an installation identity means no SLH organization or that a new administrator can be claimed |
| Local service reachable; authority uninitialized | Display **Login Administrator** for the selected LMS-SLH identity; it performs guarded first bootstrap, loopback configuration as needed, activation and signed login | Organization Service login can already succeed before bootstrap |
| Authority initialized; Organization Service `not_configured` | Authenticate locally, inspect and configure the child service | An Organization Service token is required to configure itself |
| Configured but `disabled` | Present an explicit enable decision | The Organization Service is running |
| `starting`, `failed`, `stopped_unexpectedly` or `configuration_error` | Show precise state/error, validate configuration and retry only with authorization | A positive enable response proves a listening service |
| `running` and `listening`, but no valid administrator session | Guide fresh Organization Service login | Unauthorized inventory is an empty Worker Bank |
| Valid admin session, no enrolled Workers | Show **zero enrolled Workers** as an authenticated fact and offer enrollment | Service unavailable or login failed |
| Enrolled Worker but no live managed session | Show **enrolled, not connected** and diagnose Worker-side service/identity/connectivity | Revoked, busy, or permanently missing |
| Service or session is lost during an operation | Preserve prior observations as stale, state the outcome as unknown where necessary, reauthenticate/reobserve before retries | Mutation success, rollback, or repeated safe execution without evidence |

**Returning use:** After a restart, the app again probes local status and discovers the persisted configuration rather than rerunning bootstrap. If the local supervisor is absent, it offers on-demand startup; if Organization Service is intentionally disabled, it stays disabled until explicitly enabled. Its sessions are ephemeral; previously enrolled identities and policies are not. Closing the UI must not silently disable an active service, and Windows boot must not automatically start SLH on this development machine.

### Prerequisites and existing service boundaries


**[TARGET UI]** Prerequisites for the intended complete experience: an installed Main Computer on an administrator-controlled machine, deliberate SLH configuration, protected signing credentials, suitable network access for remote Workers, recovery planning and time synchronization. Phone-based personal authentication and remote administration require separate implementation/acceptance. For SEH deployment, use the operator-approved canonical Coolify application and authenticated deployment configuration; do not manufacture another deployment identity.

**[IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** The Main Computer viewport remains loopback at `127.0.0.1:8765`. The independent local SLH control listener is `127.0.0.1:8897`. The separately enabled Organization Service has a TLS API (default `8899`) and a managed-Worker TLS/WSS listener (default `8898`); its configured network binding must be explicit and secured. These defaults supersede the old proposed `8766/TCP` listener; do not expose the local control listener or rebind the general viewport to `0.0.0.0`. On this development computer, **SLH automatic startup at Windows boot is intentionally disabled**; no UI workflow should install a boot task without explicit operator choice. Enabling the child Organization Service is a distinct authenticated action.

### 2.1 Address and name discovery

1. **[IMPLEMENTED MCEL for observation; TARGET UI for setup]** Open **Applications → Secure Local Hub** (or direct canonical `/applications/slh`) to inspect local authority status. An unconfigured or unavailable service is a legitimate application entry state, not a blocked launch. Configuration of the separate Organization Service remains a future authenticated app capability; this is not a multi-organization creation workflow.
2. **[TARGET]** Select **Network → Discover interfaces**. Show every usable assigned address, interface identifier, subnet, and link status. Automatically prefer a suitable **assigned** `10/8` address; fall back to other private ranges and offer manual selection. Never invent a `10.*` address or assume one is free.
3. **[TARGET UI]** Select the Organization Service's approved network interface and ports (the current configuration defaults are API `8899` and Worker `8898`, not `8766`). Do not bind any service to all interfaces without explicit network/firewall authorization; the local SLH control listener remains loopback-only.
4. **[TARGET]** Set **Discoverable name**. Suggested mDNS default: `mc-slh.local` on networks supporting multicast DNS. A longer friendly name such as `hub.secure.main.computer.local` is a **configurable company DNS alias** only when organizational DNS actually resolves it. `.local` is special-use multicast DNS and multi-label behavior is not universally interoperable. Show the verified IP:port fallback.
5. **[TARGET]** Press **Check reachability**. Validate binding, OS firewall, DNS/mDNS announcement, verified SLH public-key identity, and at least one independent test client. Display a specific failed gate; never silently claim discoverability on remote VPN or segmented networks.
6. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** Inspect local status, then explicitly **configure / enable / disable / restart Organization Service** using authenticated local controls. `GET /v1/local/status` is a read-only local observation; `GET /v1/local/services` and `POST /v1/local/organization/{enable,disable,restart}` are administrator actions. Distinguish `not_configured`, `disabled`, `starting`, `running`, `failed`, and `stopped_unexpectedly`. Closing the UI must not implicitly terminate an independently running service. Do not install a Windows boot-start task on this development machine.

### 2.2 Initialize the SLH public identity and Known Device registry

1. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** Open the local bootstrap flow for the single local organization; initialize protected SLH service signing identity and Known Device/user/administrator records. **Do not ask for or generate an operator-selected `org_id`.** There is no organizational CA or device CSR issuance; the service may separately use a TLS serving certificate for transport.
2. **[TARGET]** Display the SLH's public-key fingerprint to the administrator. Share it securely during enrollment by QR, USB or independently checked fingerprint; a discovered DNS name alone is not proof of identity.
3. **[CURRENT BACKEND]** First-administrator setup uses the guarded loopback bootstrap-secret flow and staged P-256 activation. **[V6-A/V6-B/V6-C IMPLEMENTED FOR ESTABLISHED ORGS]** On ORG-SLH startup, create/reuse the persistent `LOCAL-AUTH-KEY`; an authorized LMS-SLH instance reads it from disk, submits it to the network-accessible ORG-SLH API with proof of possession of the chosen ordinary identity, and explicitly registers that identity as administrator. The same grant supports recovery without resetting the organization. The current `/v1/bootstrap/recovery` and `/v1/auth/people/*` remain **[STUB]**. See [ORG-SLH Root Authorization](slh-local-auth-key-root-authorization.md).
4. **[TARGET]** Confirm the SLH listener uses established authenticated encryption, that its service public key is pinned by enrolled clients, and that the ordinary local Main Computer viewport remains loopback-only.
5. **[TARGET]** Show **Device Registry**, **New Device Authorization Queue**, **Admin Accounts**, **SEH Relays**, **Sessions**, and **Audit** in the host admin interface. The same views are available to equally authorized administrators connecting from their own SLH app.

**Failure rule:** unknown, revoked, changed-key or incorrectly authenticated devices fail closed. Replacing the SLH service identity requires explicit authorized re-verification, never automatic trust of a new key from an SEH relay.

### Local administrator login: first enrollment versus recovery (V6-C)

On a **truly unestablished** local organization, **Login Administrator** is the only first-administrator action shown: it uses the selected LMS-SLH identity to complete the guarded local bootstrap (no Admin ID, Admin Name, or P256 input), ensures a loopback TLS service configuration where none exists, activates ORG-SLH, and authenticates. When ORG-SLH is **already established and running**, the same button reads public identity selection and queries **LMS-SLH's local status API**. That status is computed by LMS-SLH from ORG-SLH's canonical local database, not by calling the remote ORG-SLH endpoint to discover the administrator count. When zero active admin identities exist, LMS-SLH submits the selected identity's signed, **bootstrap-only** root grant, ORG-SLH enforces the zero-admin condition atomically, and the button then performs a standard challenge/signature login. No separate user-run PowerShell registration command is part of this normal flow.

When one or more active administrator identities already exist, the button performs standard login only. An unregistered selected identity is refused even if earlier keys were deleted from this host; deleting a key file does not revoke the organization's enrollment record. That situation requires a **separate deliberate administrator recovery action**, not automatic login promotion. The mechanism never initializes or starts ORG-SLH merely because an identity exists or LMS-SLH starts; these changes occur only after the user clicks **Login Administrator** with a verified local identity. The selected identity has no intrinsic administrator privilege.

## 3. Enroll the administrator's authenticator and phone

### 3.1 First bootstrap and remote administration

1. **[CURRENT]** The existing one-time **Create First Administrator** path uses a loopback control plane and `bootstrap.secret`. **[TARGET]** A local operator instead uses LMS-SLH to read ORG-SLH's persistent `LOCAL-AUTH-KEY` from host disk and submit it to the intended ORG-SLH API. The API can be reached remotely, but requires root verification and identity-key proof; mere localhost access or a known identity ID is never authority.
2. **[TARGET UI]** Ask **LMS-SLH** for available identities, including the installation default, and select one; create another **ordinary identity through the same API** only when explicitly needed. **Do not invent a separate user-key type or generate the administrator key in the viewport/backend Organization Service.** Let the administrator choose a display name and use the selected identity for explicitly authorized first-admin enrollment. The current implementation supports local P-256 bootstrap/activation and Known Device authentication; a separate phone authenticator and general first-admin recovery ceremony remain planned behavior, not working service endpoints.
3. **[V6-C IMPLEMENTED]** Clicking **Login Administrator** first establishes a truly fresh organization through the existing guarded one-time bootstrap. For an already established, running organization, it triggers root-authorized first-registration **only if the local ORG-SLH enrollment state contains zero active administrator identities**. Otherwise it performs ordinary login and refuses unknown or unauthorized identities. This is not a separate command-line step. The first grant is atomic and audited. A deliberate later root-authorized recovery uses the separate explicit registration operation, never an automatic login fallback. The durable root remains available for later deliberate recovery; it is **not** removed after first enrollment or used for routine login. Normal administrator-approved enrollment remains available.
4. **[TARGET]** From any other computer, install Main Computer and select **SLH → Connect to Existing SLH**; discover an SLH over LAN/VPN or enter its SEH domain. Verify the intended SLH identity; enroll the administrator's new device through the common enrollment flow; authenticate the administrator's own signer.
5. **[TARGET UI]** A later remote SLH application should expose the same role-authorized operational information and actions as the host-local app; ordinary remote administration uses enrolled identities, while root-authorized registration requires access to the protected bearer root. **Do not claim this remote-application parity is currently accepted.** Neither UI receives hidden marketplace keys, wallet signers or Coolify tokens.

### 3.2 Android Main Computer Authenticator & Wallet

1. **[TARGET]** Choose **Add phone** from the local SLH app. Offer **QR enrollment**, a short-lived local web invitation, **USB-connected Android** transfer where supported, or **Copy signed APK to removable media** for offline delivery.
2. **[TARGET]** For an uninstalled phone, do not promise passive LAN discovery or automatic OS identification. Present a QR invitation or ask the user to connect the phone by USB; Android installation is explicitly user-approved. Never silently sideload or require debug mode for routine installation.
3. **[TARGET]** Distribute a verifiably signed APK from an authenticated source or a verified offline installer. Show package identity, version, digest/signing identity, and enrollment invitation expiry. Maintain compatibility with current Android install/developer requirements.
4. **[TARGET UI]** On the phone, open **Main Computer Authenticator & Wallet → Join organization** and independently verify the **SLH service public-key fingerprint**, not an organizational CA root fingerprint. A relay alone cannot establish trust.
5. **[TARGET]** Phone generates its own app/device authentication key in protected hardware where possible; send its public key and short-lived pairing proof. SLH administrator approves the pending enrollment request.
6. **[TARGET]** After the first identity is enrolled, policy may require an **additional independently held identity** for step-up approval. It has the same cryptographic structure; present the selected actor and assigned role. Do not share the administrator's approval credential with employees.
7. **[TARGET]** Optional: **Create wallet**, **Import wallet**, or **Skip wallet**. Phone device-auth keys and marketplace/wallet signing keys are separate. Import/export must have explicit authorization and encrypted handling.
8. **[TARGET]** Test approval: SLH presents a harmless named action; phone shows SLH identity, requested action, subject, expiry, and nonce; user confirms; SLH verifies the signed approval before executing.

The phone may later reach this SLH through LAN discovery **or** through an SEH relay. Both routes use the same previously established organizational trust.

## 4. Enroll workers, requesters, phones and administrator devices

**[IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** Device enrollment uses a protected P-256 signing keypair, an administrator-reviewed request and approval of **that exact public key**. Enrolled devices authenticate through fresh Known Device challenges and short-lived sessions. No organizational client-certificate CA is required. **[TARGET UI]** General phone/person-factor policy remains a separate future workflow; being an enrolled device alone never grants broad administration.

### 4.1 The two first-connection choices — same backend approval

1. **[TARGET]** In Worker, Requester, phone, or SLH **Connect** mode, discover or enter the SLH address and verify its public-key identity out of band.
2. **[TARGET]** The client generates its own private signing key locally; creates an enrollment request with proposed device name, user, intended type, public key and fingerprint; and shows an opt-in list of administrator display aliases.
3. **[TARGET]** Select an administrator. The UI offers **Administrator-issued enrollment key (optional)** and **Request administrator approval**.
4. **[TARGET]** With a valid one-time enrollment key scoped to the request or device public key, **Enroll with key** invokes the *same backend approval operation* as an administrator approving from the SLH queue. The key is never an administrator's reusable password or signing key.
5. **[TARGET]** Without an enrollment key, the request waits in that administrator's **New Device Authorization Queue**. The administrator may approve, deny, comment, assign an owner, or mark **Physical inspection required**. Company policy may require the administrator to log in at the device before approving it.
6. **[TARGET]** The common approval service verifies authorization and policy, consumes any key once, records administrator/method/time, and activates one immutable device identity. It then challenges the device to prove possession of its private key.
7. **[TARGET]** On later connections, device signs a fresh nonce-bound Known Device challenge. An optional additional authentication factor is required only if organization policy demands it; any cryptographic factor uses the same identity model. Enrollment of **any** identity does not itself grant administration privileges.

### 4.2 Administrator device list

**[TARGET]** Open **SLH → Devices**. Each entry includes stable ID and public-key fingerprint, editable friendly name, assigned user or unattended-machine flag, device type (worker/requester/phone/admin client), permissions, status, last seen, enrollment approver/method and **administrator notes**. Support review/approve, inspect-before-approval, disable, revoke, key rollover and role edits. Notes/labels do not establish identity. All list and mutation functions use the identical authenticated admin API from host-local and remote SLH clients.

### 4.3 Offline / USB sneaker-net

**[TARGET]** Export a short-lived invitation, SLH public identity/fingerprint and setup instructions to an automatically offered removable drive (or administrator-chosen path). The worker creates its private key locally and returns the enrollment request/public key through USB. The administrator approves using the same queue/approval API; the device imports a signed or authenticated **approval record**, not a client certificate. No administrator/private key is copied to USB. Offline storage/transport by itself does not permit performing live work; subsequent network sessions still require authenticated encryption and current policy.

### 4.4 Authentication policy after enrollment

**[TARGET]** Admin sets: unattended Worker permissions, an optional additional independently held LMS-SLH identity for step-up by role/action, session expiration, high-risk admin approval, remote SEH eligibility, physical-inspection enrollment rule, and revoke/disable behavior. Another actor must never receive the administrator's private signing key. The SEH and direct LAN paths call the same SLH verification and authorization functions.

## 5. Operate the worker pool

1. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** Open **Workers → Fleet** to inspect device identity, approval/revocation, capabilities, heartbeat, reservations, advertised public capacity and current job source. The managed Worker gateway (`/v1/managed/workers/live-session`) provides live state only while the Worker-serving Organization Service runs; the generic `/v1/workers` routes are **[STUB]** and must not be used as its source. Durable enrollment is not proof of a live Worker connection.
2. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** Inspect/change the revisioned scheduler policy using authenticated admin controls. Current defaults: `private_first`, **20% local organization retention**, **80% public ceiling** and **1 settled-credit local reserve threshold**, subject to eligibility and authorized policy changes. If reserve credit is unavailable, idle capacity may be released for public credit recovery (including the sole Worker under the documented single-Worker rule); funded local requests can reclaim eligible idle/withdrawn capacity without interrupting authorized public jobs. Always show configured policy separately from observed/possibly stale runtime state, and never promise a percentage of fractional Workers.
3. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** On **Marketplace**, inspect/bind the existing verified organization wallet and authorized Stable Hub, then configure marketplace participation and reconcile logical public Worker sessions. A wallet binding or public offer is not authorization to spend, and a public job must be Hub-authorized before execution. The Organization Service exposes authenticated `/v1/org/admin/marketplace/*` controls; generic `/v1/marketplace` routes are **[STUB]**. Physical Workers remain walletless.
4. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** On **Private Requests**, inspect approved requesters, eligible Worker models/operations, capacity admission, dispatch and outcomes. The Organization Service implements authenticated requester-role `POST /v1/work` and `GET /v1/work/{id}` when enabled; administrator pages must not impersonate the requester role. Admission requires fresh authorized credit/capacity, requests are idempotent, and an uncertain post-dispatch result is not silently replayed. Private prompts/results must not leak to the public Hub or SEH.
5. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** On **Credits → Organization Wallet**, distinguish confirmed Hub-ledger balance, pending earnings/holds, aged or missing observation, and independent transfer/spending authorization. `GET /v1/wallets`, `GET /v1/credits` and authorized reconciliation supply read-only views when a verified wallet backend exists. Even a current balance observation is **not** a wallet-spend grant. Paid public Worker activity can earn settled credits but does **not** incur a Pinky Promise payment.
6. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** On **Secure Model Access**, authenticate a requester, select an eligible organizational Worker and complete a private request. Preserve the request/fulfillment identity, selected Worker's authoritative quoted marketplace price, captured Pinky Promise policy revision and terminal outcome. The current V2 assessment uses this price evidence, **not prompt inspection or token-based repricing**. Persist result hashes and audit metadata without disclosing prompts or responses in the public accounting path.
7. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED/CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** On **Credits → Pinky Promise**, show the current default-enabled V2 policy (`local_reference_20_percent_v2`) and **20% of the selected Worker's quoted normal marketplace price**, rounded up in credit minor units, for each successfully fulfilled qualifying internal request. For example, a Worker quote of **1 credit** yields **0.2 credit** in obligation; twenty such fulfilled requests create **4 credits** total, subject to each actual quote. Show request ID, captured quote, policy revision, assessment, unpaid obligations, wallet funding, sweep state and confirmed Hub Reserve receipts. Historical obligations assessed under `fixed_per_request_v1` remain at their original **0.1-credit** amounts; they are not repriced. Public earnings themselves are not assessed.
8. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED BACKEND]** An administrator may explicitly disable the Pinky Promise **for future eligible requests**, with current revision and required consequence acknowledgment through `GET/PATCH /v1/admin/pinky-promise/policy`. Earlier obligations and their authorized settlement survive the change. Public participation remains a **separate scheduler/marketplace decision**, never an automatic consequence of toggling this accounting policy.
9. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** Reconcile fulfilled-use liabilities, verified wallet observations, pending aggregate sweeps and authoritative Stable Hub Reserve-account receipts. The Organization Service sweeper attempts startup/periodic batches every 300 seconds when Worker service is active; `GET /v1/accounting/obligations`, `GET /v1/accounting/sweeps`, and admin `POST /v1/accounting/sweep` expose accounting state/actions when authorized. Unknown outcomes, insufficient funds and unavailable Hub retain unpaid obligations; no duplicate debits or fabricated receipts. The SLH **promises** to report local use honestly; the public Hub cannot prove hidden local work occurred.

**Accounting distinction:** a fresh observed wallet balance is not independent spending authority; public earnings become available only after authoritative settlement. Mere authentication, admission or route issuance does not generate a Pinky Promise obligation. A qualifying completed private request records its V2 liability **atomically with successful fulfillment**, based on the selected Worker's normal marketplace price; failed, refused, cancelled or uncertain outcomes are not billed as success. The older [Pinky Promise V1 document](slh-pinky-promise-economy.md) preserves the fixed 0.1-credit history. Current implementation and limits are in the [backend reference](slh-backend-current-capabilities.md#9-pinky-promise--current-policy-and-settlement-boundary). **An accepted backend accounting path is not proof that these future UI controls exist.**

## 6. Provision and administer an SEH on Coolify from the local SLH application

An **SEH** is the **Secure External Hub**, an untrusted external relay operated via canonical Coolify deployment state and an [existing SEH operator harness](seh-operator-harness.md). The SLH remains local; no SLH authority or private Worker runtime is deployed to Coolify. Canonical deployment inspection/activation and local outbound connection supervision are implemented **outside the not-yet-authored SLH UI**. Generic `/v1/seh/*` and `/v1/admin/seh/{plan,deploy,start}` routes are still **[STUB]** and must not be mistaken for these tools.

### 6.1 Administrator workflow — no manual server edits required

1. **[TARGET]** Open **SLH → Remote Access → SEH Relays → Add SEH**.
2. **[TARGET UI]** Inspect and administer the **one canonical Coolify SEH application binding** using the existing operator harness and Mother/Coolify deployment identity. Where first-time provisioning is authorized, select the intended project/environment/server/domain and a scoped token stored server-side. Do not create an unrelated SEH instance or regenerate SLH identity merely because a UI cannot find the existing binding.
3. **[TARGET]** Press **Test Coolify access**. Read-only: check API reachability, token permissions, target UUIDs, proxy/HTTPS availability, DNS readiness, and image release compatibility. No resources created yet.
4. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED OPERATOR TOOL]** Inspect the current canonical SEH target and a proposed mutation separately; display source/deployment identity, immutable version/digest, redacted configuration, domain, health, and the exact authorized Coolify resource. The existing operator flow is **inspect → diagnostic → reconcile-metadata → activate-seh**; deployment mutation is not a side effect of read-only inspection.
5. **[TARGET]** Press **Deploy SEH** and approve with the administrator's authenticator. Local SLH uses the Coolify API to create/update the designated SEH resource; it must not deploy an SLH identity database, wallet service, private worker runtime, or uncontrolled ingress into the LAN.
6. **[TARGET UI, IMPLEMENTED INSPECTION]** Inspect the canonical Coolify deployment, HTTPS/TLS, public WSS route, deployment identity and actual outbound SLH tunnel state. **Deployed**, **enabled**, **connected**, and **cryptographically verified** are separate observations; neither HTTP 200 nor an activation request means a remote session is accepted.
7. **[TARGET UI, CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** Initiate an **outbound, authenticated** SLH-to-SEH connection only against the verified canonical deployment and preserved SLH public identity. Local and organization activation endpoints exist, but must be distinguished from evidence that the tunnel is connected and verified. SEH only relays authorized ciphertext; a stale Mother generation, key mismatch or missing remote application fails closed.
8. **[TARGET]** Run **SEH Harness → Test remote phone connection**: a pre-enrolled phone outside the LAN connects to `https://hub.secure.company.com`, validates its SLH's previously verified identity, authenticates independently, and performs a harmless signed request. Verify the public relay cannot forge authorizations or read private payloads.
9. **[TARGET]** Mark **Ready for remote users** only when all required SEH harness gates pass, not merely when Coolify deploys a container.
10. **[TARGET]** From SLH UI, use **Pause remote access**, **Rotate relay credentials**, **Redeploy pinned version**, **Run SEH harness**, **View sanitized logs**, and **Retire SEH**. Stop/delete actions require explicit target confirmation and must never touch unrelated Coolify resources.

The target UI deployment and recovery behaviors are detailed in [SEH Coolify Deployment and Harness](seh-secure-external-hub-coolify-harness.md). The working operator pathway is documented in [SEH Operator Harness](seh-operator-harness.md). Do not infer that target UI screens, generic API stubs or a remote-phone workflow have been implemented merely because the harness can inspect or activate an SEH.

### 6.2 SEH service-key pairing and remote administration [TARGET]

**[TARGET UI]** An authorized administrator approves an exact, canonical Coolify operation using server-held credentials. The token stays in protected local storage and never enters SEH or browser JavaScript. Coolify's public proxy terminates normal internet-facing HTTPS/WSS TLS; **SLH and managed Worker services also use TLS transport, but the organization does not operate an internal device-certificate CA.** The full remote-administrator app workflow is not yet verified.

1. **[TARGET]** Verify the exact project, server, resource UUID, domain, immutable image/config digest and required deployment permissions.
2. **[TARGET]** Deploy and start the pinned SEH service; report `RUNNING_UNPAIRED`, not `Ready`, until identity and relay tests pass.
3. **[TARGET]** Generate a one-time SEH pairing capability scoped to the expected deployment. The new SEH creates its own service keypair and submits its public key/proof of possession.
4. **[TARGET]** SLH verifies pairing, expected Coolify resource and administrator approval, and **registers the SEH public key**. SEH pins the intended SLH service public key. There is no CSR, organizational CA, or SEH client certificate.
5. **[TARGET]** SLH establishes an authenticated outbound encrypted connection and rejects unknown/revoked relay keys. Remote clients independently establish an end-to-end encrypted session with the pinned SLH identity **inside** the public HTTPS/WSS SEH path.
6. **[TARGET]** Test a remote administrator using their *own* SLH app: known-device login, personal admin signer, full role-equivalent admin API access and action-scoped audit. SEH must be unable to read admin data, grant roles, or impersonate either endpoint.

See [SLH/SEH Device Authentication, Enrollment and Remote Administration](slh-seh-device-authentication-architecture.md) for the intended bootstrap/enrollment security rules and [SEH Operator Harness](seh-operator-harness.md) for current operator controls.

If an exceptional manual repair is authorized, first inspect the existing canonical Coolify resource, its source, immutable release, deployment metadata, domain, TLS/WSS and SLH identity binding. ## 7. Day-2 operations and emergency procedures

| Situation | Administrator action | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worker lost/stolen | Revoke device public key and fence active sessions | Other Known Devices retain independent identities |
| Phone or personal authenticator lost | Revoke relevant personal/device keys; use approved secondary admin or local recovery | Lost signer cannot approve actions |
| Worker reassigned | Update user, permissions and notes through admin API | Stable cryptographic device ID retained; change audited |
| Device signing key compromised | Revoke device and require approved new enrollment/key rotation | No silent trust reuse |
| SLH service identity compromised | Local protected recovery and explicit client re-verification | SEH cannot silently replace SLH identity |
| SEH compromised | Revoke paired relay public key, disconnect tunnel, deploy/approve replacement | No ability to read E2E payload or approve users |
| Coolify API token compromised | Revoke/rotate via Coolify; update SLH secret reference | Management rights removed without changing device identities |
| USB drive lost | Expire/revoke unused invitations; generate replacement | No reusable administrator key stored on media |
| Remote admin laptop lost | Revoke its Known Device identity and sessions | Administrator account can survive and enroll a replacement under policy |
| Marketplace connection offline | Retain unpaid Pinky Promise obligations, separate observed balance from spend authority, reconcile after Hub recovery | No invented credits, silently forgiven obligations, or duplicate Reserve transfer |

**Observability [TARGET]:** SLH listener, public-key identity/fingerprint, device approval queue, person and device sessions, known-device changes, worker/public virtual sessions, SEH tunnel health, Coolify deployment IDs and sanitized audit. Never log authenticator private keys, one-time enrollment capabilities, marketplace wallet secrets, Coolify tokens or private prompts.

## 8. Definition of done

**First-run application acceptance, before expanded administration:**

- Opening `/applications/slh` without either SLH service running yields a truthful, useful application state, not a crash or a false **unconfigured** conclusion.
- A locally authorized administrator can start the loopback control service **on demand** from the intended application flow; the action cannot silently install a Windows boot task, weaken binding policy, or grant remote bootstrap access.
- A fresh installation can establish the first administrator using the authorized local-root mechanism **[TARGET]** and configure the Organization Service with explicit activation and verified listening, **without requiring an existing administrator session**. The older bootstrap-secret flow remains the current implementation until replaced.
- A returning installation preserves its authority and configuration; a stopped service, disabled child, failed child, and expired session are diagnosed separately. No setup path silently resets existing keys or enrollments.
- The administrator logs into the Organization Service under their enrolled identity and receives an app-managed, short-lived authorized session; no development-only copied bearer is needed for the completed workflow. Bearers must not enter URLs, application logs, or persistent browser storage.
- An enrolled Worker becomes visible only through authenticated observations; connection state remains distinct from enrollment. An explicitly authorized Worker mutation produces an audited backend change visible on refresh.
- Tests exercise the **unconfigured → local bootstrap → configure/enable → authenticated Worker → authorized change** path end to end, including access denial, missing services, restart and failed activation. Passing isolated tests alone does not prove this finished flow.

**Longer-term SLH acceptance:**

- **[TARGET]** ORG-SLH startup persists and reuses `LOCAL-AUTH-KEY`; root-authorized first-admin registration and later recovery require the matching root and possession of the chosen identity key. Network-origin restrictions alone cannot prevent misuse of a stolen root.
- Worker/Requester/phone/remote-admin clients generate their own device signing keys. **Enroll with key** and **admin approves from queue** execute the same policy-controlled approval code.
- Administrators can require physical presence before approval and see device name, owner, notes, roles, fingerprint, status and audit.
- The same Known Device and optional personal-authenticator challenge works directly on LAN and through SEH.
- A locally logged-in SLH administrator and equivalently privileged administrator using their own SLH application receive the same authorized backend information/actions, including SEH/Coolify and marketplace controls, without disclosing secrets.
- SEH deploys through a scoped Coolify key, pairs its service public key, relays end-to-end encrypted traffic, and cannot forge authentication or administrator approvals.
- The underlying SLH can proxy appropriately authorized logical public Worker sessions, fulfill authenticated private requests and atomically assess **20% of the selected Worker's quoted price** under V2; its configured sweeper batches liabilities into authorized, idempotent Stable Hub Reserve-account transfers. The UI must expose receipts and failure states without confusing ledger finality with external blockchain settlement. Historical 0.1-credit V1 obligations remain unchanged.
- SEH harness and managed-auth harness report real negative tests and separate mock/control-plane/live states accurately.

**Current implementation boundary:** SLH identity/enrollment and authentication, explicit local/Organization Service lifecycle, managed Worker and scheduling, private requester execution, conditional marketplace/wallet service, Pinky Promise V2 obligations/sweeps, and canonical SEH connection/harness paths are implemented in source, each subject to its runtime configuration and accepted test scope. **The MCEL SLH Overview and authenticated Worker Bank are implemented first slices**; first-run setup, service activation, in-app administrator login, Worker enrollment, complete phone administration, general personal-authenticator/recovery APIs and generic 501 stub endpoints are **not** implemented in the application. This manual does not certify a live local service, deployment, or future UI merely by documenting the workflow. See the [backend reference](slh-backend-current-capabilities.md).

## Pinky Promise periodic local bridge sweep — P4

**[IMPLEMENTED/CONDITIONAL BACKEND]** Each successfully fulfilled qualifying private request under the V2 policy accrues **20% of its selected Worker's quoted marketplace price**, rounded up in credit minor units, **without an immediate payment**. A Worker price of 1 credit implies a 0.2-credit obligation, not the historical fixed 0.1-credit contribution. The Organization Service periodically batches unpaid obligations and sends an authorized, idempotent transfer from its verified organization wallet to the configured Reserve-controlled account on the Stable Hub credit ledger; it then records the Hub receipt. Insufficient funds, missing signer, unavailable Hub and uncertain outcomes leave obligations pending rather than inventing settlement. A configured protected organization-wallet signer and an independently verified Reserve address are mandatory; an MSK alone never authorizes a debit. The receipt proves **Hub-ledger Reserve credit**, not an external on-chain transfer. [Historical P4 fixed-price documentation](slh-pinky-promise-economy.md) remains useful for earlier V1 obligations; current rules are described in [SLH Backend Current Capabilities](slh-backend-current-capabilities.md#9-pinky-promise--current-policy-and-settlement-boundary).
