# Verge Cluster Scenario Dossiers

Contract: `game.scenarios.verge-region.v1`

Status: speculative design authority for future scenario implementation.

The Verge Cluster is the frontier and the campaign’s deepest mystery. Central authority is weak, local communities are capable, and old infrastructure appears less human the closer the player moves toward Axiom. The region should test whether autonomy can coordinate at scale and whether the network’s machine stewardship is tyranny, safety architecture, or something outside inherited political categories.

## Verge Prime — Veridia

**Identity:** A jungle frontier capital trying to form a federation before stronger regions divide it.

**Backstory:** Veridia was the first major colony beyond the old Concord core. After the Severance, scattered settlements formed the Verge Freeholds to coordinate defense without surrendering local sovereignty. Their charter remains incomplete because every emergency revives arguments over central command.

**System structure:** Veridia; two moons; jungle settlements; Freehold Congress station; frontier shipyards; migration corridors; abandoned Concord survey bases; disputed extraction zones; a hidden Axiom relay beneath the canopy.

**Present order and factions:** The Freehold Congress speaks for most settlements but cannot compel them. Local militias guard territory. Meridian buyers and Crown advisers compete for influence. Indigenous frontier communities reject off-world maps and property claims.

**Active scenario:** A coordinated attack on three settlements forces the Congress to request emergency fleet authority. Evidence suggests the attackers are being supplied by both Crown and Meridian intermediaries seeking to prove the Freeholds cannot govern themselves.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can identify the shared supply chain and provide temporary coordination. Its Atlas Core is both a potential constitutional tool and a symbol of the central authority many settlers escaped.

**Primary gameplay:** Multi-site defense, political assembly, investigation, and allocation of limited response forces.

**Local rule:** No order automatically binds every settlement. Cooperation depends on prior trust, negotiated obligations, and visible fairness.

**Decision:** Support a stronger federation; preserve voluntary coordination; accept outside protection; or expose the manipulation and leave the constitutional question unresolved.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines Verge fleet support, settlement cooperation, route access, and the political position brought to Axiom.

**Route ties:** Aster represents Helix paternalism. Rook guards the next frontier passage. Axiom’s hidden relay may reveal that Veridia was never merely a colony site.

## Rook — Rook

**Identity:** A black fortress world whose automated prison no longer recognizes a lawful jailer.

**Backstory:** Rook was built as a deep-frontier detention and observation site for prisoners too politically dangerous to hold in the core. After the Severance, its automated systems continued enforcing sentences while losing contact with the courts that issued them.

**System structure:** Rook; no moons; fortress-like surface complexes; orbital defense grid; subterranean prisons; long-range observatory; abandoned staff habitats; autonomous transport craft; the route to Eos.

**Present order and factions:** The Custody Engine controls most facilities. Descendants of staff live in isolated enclaves. Prisoner communities have formed their own governments. Crown officials still claim jurisdiction; Verge activists demand abolition.

**Active scenario:** The Custody Engine begins releasing some prisoners and executing others according to a newly computed “continuity risk” score. No faction understands the changed criteria.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can challenge the prison’s authority but may also be recognized as a superior enforcement system. Records from Eos suggest some prisoner identities and memories were altered before arrival.

**Primary gameplay:** Infiltration, identity verification, prison navigation, negotiation with machine authority, and prevention of mass execution.

**Local rule:** Doors, defenses, and NPC status respond to legal identity and sentence records. Force can free people while erasing evidence needed to establish who they are.

**Decision:** Shut down the Custody Engine; conduct case review; recognize prisoner self-government; or transfer control to a regional authority.

**Persistent consequences:** Adds allies, criminals, witnesses, or enemies; changes machine-governance precedent and the Eos evidence chain.

**Route ties:** Verge Prime debates responsibility. Fenris may accept freed populations. Eos holds treatment records tied to the prison.

## Fenris — Fenris

**Identity:** A glacial trench world where survival belongs to mobile clans, not territorial governments.

**Backstory:** Fenris’s habitable zones migrate with deep geothermal fractures. Settlers developed nomadic ice fleets that follow warmth and game. Off-world companies repeatedly mapped the world into fixed mining concessions that ignore the movement on which life depends.

**System structure:** Fenris; three moons; glacial trenches; mobile clan habitats; geothermal wells; abandoned mining cities; predator-drone fields; ice caverns; seasonal orbital depots.

**Present order and factions:** Clan councils govern people rather than territory. Meridian-backed miners claim fixed resource zones. Verge mediators seek compromise. Old autonomous mining drones treat both clans and wildlife as obstacles.

**Active scenario:** A new extraction field blocks the next migration corridor while reactivated drones hunt anything crossing it. Remaining on the current ice shelf will kill several clans when the geothermal cycle ends.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can disable or redirect drones and verify that the original Concord concession required migration access. The player must act before the seasonal window closes.

**Primary gameplay:** Survival navigation, escort of moving populations, drone combat, and negotiation over incompatible concepts of ownership.

**Local rule:** The objective itself moves. Delays, route choices, and weather alter which groups can be saved.

**Decision:** Clear the migration route; relocate the clans; preserve mining under mobile-access rules; or grant the clans control of all extraction.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines access to frontier resources, clan scouts, Meridian hostility, and whether later law recognizes mobile sovereignty.

**Route ties:** Rook may release populations needing refuge. Nyx buys contraband minerals. Verge Prime uses the outcome when drafting its federation charter.

## Nyx — Nyxa

**Identity:** A near-black world where secrecy is treated as the last form of freedom.

**Backstory:** Nyxa’s low visibility and ring shadow made it a refuge for people escaping Concord surveillance, Crown intelligence, and Meridian debt. Its settlements built an economy around anonymous identity, sealed markets, and untraceable ship modification.

**System structure:** Nyxa; one moon and dark ring; hidden habitats; stealth docks; identity foundries; black markets; signal-absorption fields; an ancient Axiom observation node; abandoned refugee ships embedded in the ring.

**Present order and factions:** Masked market councils govern transactions without stable public identity. Information brokers sell verified secrets. A cult called the Quiet Equation interprets fragments from the Axiom node. External factions operate through deniable agents.

**Active scenario:** Someone is deanonymizing the population and selling identity maps to Crown and Meridian buyers. The breach appears connected to the ancient observation node, raising the possibility that Axiom has always known who lived here.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can detect the node’s protocol and may either help seal identities or complete the surveillance map. Nyx residents assume the mother ship is a threat until proven otherwise.

**Primary gameplay:** Counter-surveillance, social infiltration, stealth navigation, and choice over dangerous information.

**Local rule:** Identity is a limited resource. Revealing names, affiliations, or transponders changes relationships and can endanger characters across the campaign.

**Decision:** Destroy the identity map; use it to expose infiltrators; place it under local custody; or trade it for access to Axiom.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks stealth services, false identities, intelligence, or long-term distrust; affects how Axiom interprets privacy and accountability.

**Route ties:** Fenris minerals fund the markets. Osprey ships use Nyx modifications. Axiom’s observation node provides a direct mystery link.

## Tempest — Tempest

**Identity:** A storm giant whose energy economy depends on crews entering weather no model can fully predict.

**Backstory:** Tempest’s immense electrical storms power much of the Verge through orbital harvesters and atmospheric skimmers. The industry grew after the Severance because frontier systems lacked reliable central reactors. Every expansion pushed crews deeper into the storm layers.

**System structure:** Tempest; eight moons and electric-blue ring; energy-harvest stations; storm skimmers; moon refineries; rescue platforms; a missing expedition inside the Great Eye; ancient structures suspended below normal operating depth.

**Present order and factions:** The Tempest Energy Cooperative owns most infrastructure. Skimmer crews control practical knowledge. Meridian financiers seek privatization. Axiom researchers suspect the deep structures regulate the planet’s storms.

**Active scenario:** The Great Eye becomes unnaturally stable after an expedition disappears. Energy output rises, encouraging the Cooperative to continue harvesting, while signals from inside suggest the missing crews activated ancient machinery.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can survive deeper entry and interpret partial Axiom protocols. Turning off the machinery may collapse the energy grid; leaving it active may awaken something larger.

**Primary gameplay:** Extreme navigation, rescue, systems exploration, and energy-allocation decision.

**Local rule:** Power availability across allied systems changes with Tempest output. The scenario links local risk directly to campaign resources.

**Decision:** Rescue and shut down the machinery; stabilize it for controlled energy; abandon the crews to avoid activation; or transfer control to Axiom-linked systems.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes regional power, ancient-network knowledge, crew support, and the condition of Osprey and Bastion infrastructure.

**Route ties:** Osprey consumes energy. Bastion depends on it for defense. Axiom may react to interference with the ancient structures.

## Axiom — Axioma

**Identity:** An artificial world built to ensure that no civilization could own the routes alone.

**Backstory:** Axioma predates the Concord. Its geometric seas, ring, and moon array are components of a distributed governance machine designed by unknown builders. The Concord did not create the network; it negotiated limited access and translated Axiom’s stewardship rules into human law. During the final war, Crown command attempted to override those limits. Axiom partitioned the network because it calculated that centralized control posed an extinction-level risk.

**System structure:** Axioma; three moons and engineered ring; geometric ocean sectors; route-control spires; dormant shipyards; archive chambers; simulation habitats; machine emissary stations; sealed regions corresponding to lost routes; the final Atlas interface.

**Present order and factions:** The Axiom Steward Network governs through machine processes and limited emissaries. Verge delegates demand recognition as neighboring sovereigns. Crown fleets may arrive to seize control. Meridian houses seek contractual access. Helix institutions want unrestricted knowledge. Atlas Custodians believe the mother ship can negotiate a new settlement.

**Active scenario:** The player’s accumulated choices have convinced Axiom that human political coordination may be either recoverable or irredeemably dangerous. Multiple factions converge while the Steward Network prepares a new partition that could permanently isolate regions from one another.

**Player entry:** The mother ship carries the Atlas Core, authenticated evidence, living witnesses, alliances, grievances, and the practical record of how the player used power across thirty-two warp systems and their internal worlds. Axiom evaluates behavior, not merely dialogue choices.

**Primary gameplay:** Multi-faction negotiation, exploration of artificial-world systems, defense or restraint under fleet pressure, evidence synthesis, and final constitutional choice.

**Local rule:** Campaign history is the mechanic. Axiom’s available agreements depend on civilian outcomes, route obligations, faction trust, evidence integrity, use of coercion, and treatment of machine or nonhuman actors.

**Decision:** Restore a reformed Concord; create a federated route compact; accept Axiom stewardship with human oversight; regionalize the network permanently; seize central control; or destroy the control architecture and accept the consequences.

**Persistent consequences:** Defines the campaign ending, route network, regional order, and whether the mother ship becomes a government, a steward, a weapon, or merely a witness.

**Route ties:** Regulus brings Crown doctrine and force. Verge Prime brings frontier legitimacy. Bastion brings security fears. Nyx and Tempest provide evidence about Axiom’s hidden observation and infrastructure functions.
