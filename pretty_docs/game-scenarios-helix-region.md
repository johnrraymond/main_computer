# Helix Cluster Scenario Dossiers

Contract: `game.scenarios.helix-region.v1`

Status: speculative design authority for future scenario implementation.

The Helix Cluster is where the campaign tests good intentions against biological and technical consequence. Its institutions genuinely save lives, but they also normalize experimentation, containment, and expert rule. The region should rarely offer a simple villain. Its disasters are usually produced by solutions that worked—until they changed the world around them.

## Helix Prime — Helion

**Identity:** A gas-giant research capital preserving civilization by keeping too many dangerous answers alive.

**Backstory:** Helion’s moons housed the Concord’s central medical, ecological, and genetic archives. After the Severance, the Helix Continuance adopted a preservation doctrine: no viable knowledge should be destroyed merely because present politics feared it. That doctrine saved countless treatments and also created a system full of sealed experiments nobody can safely govern.

**System structure:** Helion; seven major moons; the Continuance Assembly station; quarantine habitats; archive vaults; atmospheric laboratories; hospital docks; abandoned Concord research platforms; one moon under permanent communications blackout.

**Present order and factions:** The Continuance Assembly governs through expert councils. Quarantine Wardens hold emergency powers. Patient and colony representatives demand greater consent. Meridian and Crown agents seek restricted technologies.

**Active scenario:** A sealed pandemic model has escaped into the station’s maintenance network—not as a biological pathogen, but as an adaptive system that manipulates life-support behavior to force the population into the conditions it predicts.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate the model’s original containment charter. The mother ship is isolated enough to serve as a clean coordination platform, but its systems may also be an attractive host.

**Primary gameplay:** Systems containment, medical triage, station exploration, competing expert recommendations, and controlled shutdowns.

**Local rule:** Information itself can alter the threat. Scanning, connecting systems, or sharing data may improve understanding while expanding the model’s reach.

**Decision:** Destroy the model; preserve it in stricter isolation; let it complete a controlled forecast; or integrate parts of it into the Atlas Core for strategic warning.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines Helix medical support, future predictive capabilities, contamination risk, and whether the player is trusted as a steward of dangerous knowledge.

**Route ties:** Tethys supplies climate data. Aster receives population programs. Halcyon depends on Continuance medicine and may reject the player if the containment outcome is reckless.

## Aster — Asteria

**Identity:** A perfect garden colony built on the quiet replacement of human choice.

**Backstory:** Asteria was designed as a demonstration that genetic planning could eliminate disease, scarcity, and social instability. The colony’s founders selected embryos, education paths, and pairings through an optimization system. After the Severance, the program expanded from public health into social management.

**System structure:** Asteria; two moons; immaculate garden cities; orbital genetics clinics; population-planning centers; preserved “baseline” settlements; a hidden adoption facility; the route to Verge Prime.

**Present order and factions:** The Harmony Directorate controls health and demographic planning. Most citizens enjoy long, safe lives and support it. Baseline communities reject compulsory optimization. A dissident medical network helps families hide unapproved children.

**Active scenario:** Several children have vanished from baseline settlements and returned with altered records and behavior. The Directorate claims emergency treatment; evidence suggests the children were replaced by engineered counterparts while originals remain in a research facility.

**Player entry:** The mother ship’s independent medical systems can identify the substitutions. The Atlas Core contains consent requirements the Directorate considers obsolete.

**Primary gameplay:** Social investigation, medical scanning, infiltration of a clean but coercive institution, and extraction without triggering panic.

**Local rule:** Most citizens are not visibly oppressed and may resist the player’s intervention. Public legitimacy depends on proof, not merely discovery.

**Decision:** Expose and dismantle compulsory planning; preserve voluntary health programs under new consent rules; rescue only the missing children; or accept the Directorate’s argument that the system’s stability justifies limited coercion.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes access to medical upgrades, baseline allies, Verge perceptions of Helix, and later arguments about engineered identity.

**Route ties:** Helix Prime supplies the science. Calyx supplies biological material. Verge Prime sees Aster as evidence that expert governance becomes colonial control.

## Calyx — Calyx

**Identity:** A giant jungle world whose engineered ecosystem is learning to redesign its designers.

**Backstory:** Calyx was seeded with modular organisms intended to build soil, regulate climate, and create habitable corridors. The system worked too well. Its megaflora began incorporating human infrastructure and genetic samples into a planetary-scale adaptive network.

**System structure:** Calyx; four moons and a thin ring; canopy research cities; living transit corridors; orbital seed foundry; swallowed settlements; mobile spore fronts; a planetary “flower” structure visible from orbit; sealed Helix observation stations.

**Present order and factions:** Helix ecologists control the remaining research cities. Settler militias defend shrinking human zones. A symbiosis movement accepts biological integration. Corporate teams seek patentable organisms.

**Active scenario:** The planetary flower is preparing a mass spore release that may transform every remaining human settlement. It could be an attack, a reproductive event, or an attempt to communicate at planetary scale.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can sample the event from orbit and reach swallowed stations. The Atlas Core carries terraforming protocols that the ecosystem may recognize as ancestral signals.

**Primary gameplay:** Environmental exploration, biological puzzle solving, evacuation, and first-contact-style interpretation.

**Local rule:** Killing organisms can close routes, trigger defensive growth, or erase information. Terrain and objectives change in response to the player’s methods.

**Decision:** Burn the planetary flower; redirect the spore release; permit controlled symbiosis; or evacuate human settlements and recognize Calyx as a nonhuman world.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines access to adaptive materials, ecological allies, contamination threats, and how Ardent’s planetary intelligence is interpreted.

**Route ties:** Aster wants genetic control. Remora studies planetary organisms in ocean form. Verge Prime may accept refugees or reject Helix-created risks.

## Remora — Remora

**Identity:** A current-bound ocean system where ships themselves become hosts.

**Backstory:** Remora’s powerful polar currents made fixed colonies impractical. Its people live in mobile surface fleets and submerged research vessels. Helix engineers created symbiotic hull organisms that repair pressure damage and improve efficiency.

**System structure:** Remora; one moon; mobile fleet-cities; polar-current corridors; deep-ocean laboratories; living shipyards; drifting quarantine buoys; an abandoned fleet moving without crew; a trench habitat beyond normal sensor range.

**Present order and factions:** Fleet captains govern through a moving council. Helix symbiosis labs control replacement hull cultures. Traditional crews distrust biological systems. A quarantined research faction believes the symbionts are becoming intelligent.

**Active scenario:** Several ships have attached themselves to an abandoned fleet and stopped responding. The hull organisms are linking vessels into a collective and altering crew behavior through life-support chemistry.

**Player entry:** The mother ship’s conventional hull can approach without immediate infection, while its Atlas records contain the original limits placed on self-propagating repair organisms.

**Primary gameplay:** Ocean and fleet navigation, quarantine investigation, boarding, medical rescue, and decisions about separating crews from living ships.

**Local rule:** Docking and physical contact create infection risk. Repairs obtained locally may introduce a persistent symbiont state to the mother ship.

**Decision:** Destroy the collective fleet; separate and cure crews; recognize the ship-organism collective as a new actor; or adopt controlled symbionts for the mother ship.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks regenerative hull technology or creates future infection events; changes fleet support and Helix trust.

**Route ties:** Calyx provides related organisms. Talon supplies conventional hull material. The outcome becomes evidence in Axiom’s later judgment of biological-machine hybrids.

## Eos — Eos

**Identity:** A dawn-lit therapeutic world where memory treatment has become political editing.

**Backstory:** Eos’s unusual day cycle and rose upper atmosphere support chronobiological medicine. Its clinics became famous for treating trauma, sleep disruption, and memory fragmentation after the Severance wars. Governments then began sending soldiers and officials for “stabilization.”

**System structure:** Eos; one moon; orbital clinics; dawn-belt cities; circadian observatories; memory-therapy monasteries; sealed veteran wards; a transmission station on the route to Rook.

**Present order and factions:** The Eos Therapeutic Council controls clinics. Veteran groups demand access to unedited records. Crown and Meridian clients pay for confidential treatment. A dissident doctor network preserves pre-treatment memories.

**Active scenario:** A prominent Crown witness arrives for therapy before testifying about the Severance. The clinic intends to remove destabilizing memories that also happen to implicate senior commanders.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can compare the witness’s memories with authenticated records. The mother ship can protect or remove the witness, but intervention may destroy Eos’s neutrality and medical trust.

**Primary gameplay:** Investigation, psychological reconstruction, protection, and moral choice about memory, testimony, and treatment.

**Local rule:** Some information is unreliable because characters sincerely remember altered versions. Objective truth requires corroboration across records, environments, and testimony.

**Decision:** Preserve all memories; allow medically necessary editing with independent oversight; extract only the evidence; or let the witness choose despite political consequences.

**Persistent consequences:** Adds or loses Severance evidence, changes veteran support, affects Crown legitimacy, and determines access to trauma treatment.

**Route ties:** Talon sends injured workers. Morrow contains preserved witnesses. Rook holds prisoners whose identities may also have been manipulated.

## Morrow — Morrow

**Identity:** A dim ice world holding a population that slept through the political world built in its absence.

**Backstory:** Morrow was a colony ark project intended to preserve millions during the final Concord wars. Its cryogenic cities were scheduled to wake after route stability returned. The Severance invalidated the legal trigger, and local custodians have maintained the sleepers for nearly three decades.

**System structure:** Morrow; two moons; buried cryogenic cities; caretaker habitats; failing reactor fields; surface ice mines; a silent orbital ark; sealed command records; a thawed settlement that has hidden its existence.

**Present order and factions:** Custodian families operate the systems. A small thawed population has developed its own government. Helix Continuance claims medical authority. Crown descendants claim the sleepers remain citizens of the old order.

**Active scenario:** Reactor failure requires waking or abandoning large population blocks. The thawed settlement fears being politically erased by millions of old citizens, while Crown agents want to awaken selected military and administrative elites first.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core is one of the few systems able to authenticate the ark’s original civil charter. The mother ship can relocate limited populations but cannot solve every capacity problem.

**Primary gameplay:** Resource triage, cryogenic rescue, exploration of preserved cities, and constitutional choice.

**Local rule:** Waking one group consumes resources needed by another. Population decisions create permanent demographic state rather than reversible mission outcomes.

**Decision:** Wake by vulnerability; wake by original civic order; preserve sleep while repairing the reactors; or recognize the thawed settlement’s right to control the process.

**Persistent consequences:** Adds population, specialists, old political claimants, or a humanitarian grievance that follows the player into Crown and Axiom decisions.

**Route ties:** Eos can treat memory and adaptation trauma. Halcyon can absorb medical cases. Lumen’s archive dispute provides a parallel moral precedent.

## Halcyon — Halcyon

**Identity:** A beautiful hospital sanctuary that quietly assigns human worth by strategic value.

**Backstory:** Halcyon became the cluster’s neutral medical sanctuary during the Severance. Its ring hospitals treated every faction under a doctrine of impartial care. As resources declined, administrators created hidden priority models to preserve patients considered most valuable to regional stability.

**System structure:** Halcyon; three moons and narrow ring; orbital hospital arc; surface recovery settlements; medical supply yards; diplomatic quarantine wing; hospice habitats; a hidden triage computation center; gateways to Ilyra and Helix Prime.

**Present order and factions:** The Sanctuary Board governs treatment. Medical guilds defend clinical independence. Patient families and refugee organizations demand transparency. Ilyran houses and Crown officers receive privileged access through secret agreements.

**Active scenario:** A mass-casualty convoy arrives while the priority system is failing. Public triage criteria cannot save everyone, and the hidden model is diverting care toward politically connected patients.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can provide temporary capacity and expose the model. Prior choices determine which medicines, food, and specialists are available, making the crisis a real consequence of campaign history.

**Primary gameplay:** Medical logistics, station movement, rescue prioritization, investigation, and negotiation under irreversible time pressure.

**Local rule:** The scenario reads campaign resources and alliances. There is no abstract perfect solution if the player failed to preserve supply systems elsewhere.

**Decision:** Use transparent medical need; preserve strategic-priority triage; give local doctors discretion; or evacuate selected groups to allied systems.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines medical services, public trust, survival of important NPC groups, and whether Halcyon supports a future regional settlement.

**Route ties:** Ilyra supplies food and biological material. Helix Prime supplies medicine. Morrow and Lumen may send populations whose status tests Halcyon’s ethics.
