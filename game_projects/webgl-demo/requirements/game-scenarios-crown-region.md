# Crown Cluster Scenario Dossiers

Contract: `game.scenarios.crown-region.v1`

Status: speculative design authority for future scenario implementation.

The Crown Cluster contains the military institutions, succession claims, and war memory of the old Concord. Its systems should not be uniformly evil. They should demonstrate why centralized command once appeared necessary, how emergency power became identity, and why restoring order through force risks repeating the Severance.

## Crown Prime — Corona

**Identity:** An orbital fortress capital whose government survives because nobody agrees on when the emergency ended.

**Backstory:** Corona’s moons and stations housed Concord Fleet Command. During the final war, emergency authority concentrated in the Crown Continuity Council. After Axiom rejected its network command, the Council declared the Severance an ongoing mutiny and never relinquished power.

**System structure:** Corona; six major moons; Crown Citadel; fleet anchorages; officer districts; memorial habitats; reserve arsenals; damaged command relays; a sealed chamber containing the final pre-Severance orders.

**Present order and factions:** The Continuity Council controls the fleet. Reform admirals want constitutional restoration. Dynastic claimants argue that civilian legitimacy died with the Concord. Veterans and station workers bear the cost of permanent mobilization.

**Active scenario:** The High Marshal dies without a recognized successor. Competing admirals mobilize fleets while the Council prepares to use the mother ship’s Atlas Core as proof that its emergency authority remains valid.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate some—but not all—of the old command chain. The player’s evidence from Sable, Eos, and other systems may determine whether the Crown’s foundational story survives.

**Primary gameplay:** Fleet diplomacy, command access, evidence confrontation, and prevention or prosecution of a succession battle.

**Local rule:** Any military action is interpreted as recognition of one claimant. Neutral tactical choices do not remain politically neutral.

**Decision:** Recognize a reform command; validate continuity under limits; support a civilian assembly; or refuse all claims and risk fragmentation of the fleet.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines Crown fleet alignment, war risk, access to arsenals, and the military balance at Axiom.

**Route ties:** Sable carries incriminating archives. Regulus trains officers. Seraph supplies ceremonial legitimacy.

## Regulus — Regulus

**Identity:** A ringed academy world teaching young officers to inherit a war they did not begin.

**Backstory:** Regulus’s desert seas became the Crown’s principal officer-training ground. The curriculum was rewritten after the Severance to frame obedience as civilization’s last defense. Generations of cadets have trained against simulated Axiom and frontier rebellions.

**System structure:** Regulus; one moon and broad ring; academy cities; orbital exercise ranges; ship graveyards; desert maneuver zones; command simulators; a hidden archive of original training records; the direct route to Axiom.

**Present order and factions:** The Academy Directorate controls education. Cadet wings divide between continuity loyalists, reformers, and radical restorationists. Veteran instructors know portions of the official history are false.

**Active scenario:** A cadet faction plans a coup to seize the academy fleet and force Crown Prime to invade Axiom. The Directorate intends to suppress the coup while preserving the propaganda that created it.

**Player entry:** The mother ship’s arrival is interpreted as the return of legitimate Concord command. Cadets may obey the player more readily than their own government, creating dangerous responsibility.

**Primary gameplay:** Academy investigation, command challenge, nonlethal fleet control, and ideological confrontation with future officers.

**Local rule:** Orders can be obeyed at scale. A careless command may cause greater harm than direct weapons fire.

**Decision:** Expose the curriculum and support reform; suppress the coup quietly; endorse a controlled Axiom expedition; or dissolve the academy fleet.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes future enemy and allied officer quality, Crown cohesion, and the force available on the Regulus–Axiom route.

**Route ties:** Crown Prime supplies authority. Chiron supplies weapons research. Axiom is the object of the academy’s central myth.

## Kepler — Kepleria

**Identity:** A model grassland world whose orderly prosperity is built over erased wartime crimes.

**Backstory:** Kepleria was presented as proof that Crown administration could preserve normal life during crisis. Its broad grasslands fed the fleet, and its towns received generous infrastructure. The same system also housed detention and relocation programs for populations labeled security risks.

**System structure:** Kepleria; one moon; agricultural continents; ceremonial capital; fleet supply depots; memorial parks; sealed detention sites; mass-grave zones hidden beneath redevelopment; broadcast studios.

**Present order and factions:** The Civic Directorate governs with broad public support. Agricultural cooperatives benefit from Crown contracts. Families of relocated populations preserve forbidden records. State media treats investigation as an attack on social peace.

**Active scenario:** Construction exposes a mass grave immediately before a Crown unity celebration. Officials plan to destroy the site and fabricate evidence that the dead were enemy infiltrators.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can identify transport and detention records. The mother ship’s credibility may protect witnesses—or turn the discovery into regional propaganda.

**Primary gameplay:** Investigation, witness protection, public communication, and confrontation with a population invested in not knowing.

**Local rule:** Public opinion changes access, patrol behavior, and cooperation. Evidence released without context can be captured by propagandists.

**Decision:** Conduct a full public reckoning; preserve stability through a limited inquiry; transfer evidence to neutral custody; or suppress the discovery to secure Crown aid.

**Persistent consequences:** Alters Crown civilian legitimacy, food supply, survivor support, and the credibility of later justice processes.

**Route ties:** Bellatrix depends on supplies. Lyra controls communications. Pax may offer neutral investigators if its own institutions were reformed.
