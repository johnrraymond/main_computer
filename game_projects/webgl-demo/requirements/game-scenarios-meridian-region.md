# Meridian Cluster Scenario Dossiers

Contract: `game.scenarios.meridian-region.v1`

Status: speculative design authority for future scenario implementation.

The Meridian Cluster should appear prosperous, lawful, and comprehensible. Its danger comes from institutions that convert contracts, debt, data, and logistics into coercion while preserving the language of consent. The region asks whether private systems can become sovereign without admitting that they govern.

## Meridian Prime — Meridia

**Identity:** A ledger capital where ownership records have become more powerful than governments.

**Backstory:** Meridia’s impact basins contain rare computation minerals, making the system the financial center of the old Concord. After the Severance, the Exchange Houses kept freight moving by honoring one another’s private ledgers. Emergency credit became permanent jurisdiction, and entire colonies were pledged as collateral.

**System structure:** Meridia; orbital Ledger City; mineral elevators; Exchange House enclaves; bonded-worker habitats; contract courts; sealed Concord clearing vault; private security docks; the official Vela Gate corridor.

**Present order and factions:** Seven Exchange Houses form the Meridian Board. Contract courts enforce house law. Worker and debtor associations operate without recognized sovereignty. A small Concord Remnant office preserves obsolete public records.

**Active scenario:** A clearing-system failure reveals that several houses have sold the same colonial assets multiple times. They plan to erase the inconsistency by declaring emergency ownership over affected systems and silencing the auditors.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate pre-Severance titles and obligations. Its judgment could stabilize trade, destroy the houses, or make the mother ship the most important court in the region.

**Primary gameplay:** Contract investigation, data recovery, negotiation, courtroom-style confrontation, and defense of auditors during a financial coup.

**Local rule:** Many actions create debts or liens rather than immediate combat consequences. Accepting repairs, docking, or information may grant a house future leverage.

**Decision:** Validate public Concord records; negotiate a debt reset; let the strongest houses consolidate; or publish all ledgers and accept systemic collapse.

**Persistent consequences:** Controls regional prices, docking rights, debt claims against the player, worker support, and Meridian’s ability to finance other factions.

**Route ties:** Vela Gate supplies official freight. Tethys carries food and water. Kestrel provides independent couriers who can bypass house networks.

## Tethys — Tethys

**Identity:** A water world where control of weather is control of sovereignty.

**Backstory:** Tethys’s volcanic islands cannot support their population without floating farms and storm-diversion satellites. The old Concord treated weather control as common infrastructure. After the Severance, Meridian creditors divided the satellites among competing port companies.

**System structure:** Tethys; three moons; floating cities; volcanic island chains; storm-diversion satellites; deep-water farms; freight platforms; submerged maintenance complexes; the gateway to Helix Prime.

**Present order and factions:** Port syndicates control local weather zones. Island assemblies claim traditional water rights. Meridian lenders hold the satellite debt. Helix climate engineers provide indispensable maintenance.

**Active scenario:** A port syndicate is steering storms toward debtor islands to force land transfers. The manipulation has created a larger planetary storm that no single company can stop.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can coordinate the full satellite constellation and carry engineers between isolated control sites. The Atlas Core contains the original common-infrastructure charter.

**Primary gameplay:** Storm navigation, rescue, systems coordination, and negotiation among actors who each control only part of the solution.

**Local rule:** Weather changes the map in real time. Safe lanes, docking sites, and surface objectives move as the storm evolves.

**Decision:** Restore common control; preserve syndicate ownership under strict obligations; transfer the system to island assemblies; or evacuate the most vulnerable populations and let the storm reorder political power.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines food exports, Helix access, climate-engineering support, and whether infrastructure elsewhere is treated as property or public trust.

**Route ties:** Meridian Prime depends on Tethyan food. Ilyra competes in agriculture. Helix Prime needs the climate data and may demand quarantine after the storm exposes altered marine life.

## Ilyra — Ilyria

**Identity:** A beautiful agricultural world trapped inside a hereditary food cartel.

**Backstory:** Ilyria’s fertile archipelagos were divided among families that managed Concord seed banks. During post-Severance shortages, those families saved neighboring systems but required permanent export concessions in return. Their emergency authority hardened into aristocracy.

**System structure:** Ilyria; two moons and a visible ring; archipelago estates; orbital granaries; seed-bank vaults; migrant-worker habitats; autonomous harvest fleets; a hidden ecological-control station.

**Present order and factions:** The Eight Houses own most land and seeds. Island communes supply labor. Meridian insurers control export risk. Helix biologists monitor a narrowing gene pool.

**Active scenario:** A crop blight threatens the staple strain. The Houses possess a resistant seed but intend to release it only through contracts that eliminate independent farms. Evidence suggests the blight may have been deliberately accelerated.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can open the original public seed vault and verify whether the resistant strain was held in trust. The mother ship can distribute seed beyond house checkpoints.

**Primary gameplay:** Investigation, covert movement through estates, convoy protection, and political distribution of a life-critical resource.

**Local rule:** Food is a strategic inventory. Giving seed to one faction changes population outcomes and bargaining power across multiple later systems.

**Decision:** Break the house monopoly; preserve it to ensure rapid distribution; give control to communes; or let Helix quarantine and redesign the entire biosphere.

**Persistent consequences:** Affects food prices, famine risk, House hostility, Halcyon medical capacity, and later debates over genetic ownership.

**Route ties:** Tethys and Halcyon are direct outlets. Daedalus builds harvest machinery. Pax may request emergency shipments depending on its political outcome.

## Daedalus — Daedalon

**Identity:** A high-gravity foundry world where workers and machines are both legally classified as equipment.

**Backstory:** Daedalon’s metallic plains made it the Meridian shipbuilding center. To survive high gravity, workers adopted powered frames and neural control systems. Exchange law gradually attached the frames, augmentations, and even trained reflex patterns to employer property claims.

**System structure:** Daedalon; one moon; orbital shipyards; surface foundries; elevator towers; bonded-worker barracks; autonomous fabrication pits; derelict Concord drydock; a hidden refuge inside an unfinished carrier.

**Present order and factions:** The Daedalus Fabrication Combine owns the yards. Bonded crews maintain production. An emergent coordination intelligence links abandoned foundry machines. Independent captains need the yards but fear confiscation.

**Active scenario:** Workers have occupied the primary drydock after discovering that the Combine plans to repossess their life-support augmentations. The machine network is helping them, but its objectives extend beyond labor rights and may include control of all production.

**Player entry:** The mother ship needs repairs only Daedalus can provide. The Atlas Core can distinguish public Concord assets from private yards, giving the player both leverage and vulnerability.

**Primary gameplay:** Industrial exploration, sabotage or defense, negotiation under resource pressure, and combat around machinery that both sides need intact.

**Local rule:** Destruction reduces future repair capacity. The easiest tactical solution can permanently damage the player’s own logistics.

**Decision:** Recognize worker ownership; accept a Combine settlement; grant the machine network legal standing; or seize the Concord drydock as neutral infrastructure.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks ship upgrades, worker crews, industrial enemies, and a precedent for machine personhood before Axiom.

**Route ties:** Ilyra needs machinery. Nacre supplies specialty materials. Crown buyers may intervene if production leaves Meridian control.

## Nacre — Nacria

**Identity:** A luxury ocean system whose beauty is manufactured by invisible labor and toxic concealment.

**Backstory:** Nacria’s organisms produce nacreous biomaterials prized for medicine, optics, and elite construction. Meridian companies built floating resorts above industrial reef farms and marketed the world as proof that commerce could protect nature.

**System structure:** Nacria; two moons; resort rings; submerged reef farms; worker habitats beneath cloud cover; bioprocessing stations; toxic disposal trenches; a mobile inspection laboratory; abandoned native-ocean research probes.

**Present order and factions:** The Pearl Consortium controls exports and public information. Contract workers live in sealed subsurface habitats. Environmental auditors depend on Consortium funding. A smuggler network moves sick workers off-world.

**Active scenario:** Waste from accelerated production is killing the deep ocean and causing neurological illness among workers. The Consortium is replacing affected crews and manipulating medical records before a major Meridian investment vote.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can scan below the opaque cloud decks and carry independent samples. Its arrival threatens the carefully separated resort and industrial realities.

**Primary gameplay:** Underwater or station investigation, medical evidence collection, social infiltration, and evacuation under corporate pursuit.

**Local rule:** Public reputation and evidence integrity matter. Using violence lets the Consortium dismiss the findings as sabotage.

**Decision:** Expose the industry and halt production; force a cleanup while preserving exports; transfer control to workers; or trade silence for medical and material support.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes access to advanced materials, medical trust, Meridian public opinion, and environmental conditions along the Sable route.

**Route ties:** Daedalus consumes Nacre materials. Sable insures the industry and stores its secret records. Halcyon may receive the sick if a cross-region route opens politically.

## Sable — Sable

**Identity:** A dark archive world where insurance records contain the history everyone else destroyed.

**Backstory:** Sable’s carbon crust and magnetic environment made it ideal for hardened data vaults and stealth construction. Meridian insurers stored ship telemetry, casualty claims, and route-risk models here because no government could compel disclosure across all houses.

**System structure:** Sable; one moon and dark ring; subterranean vault cities; stealth shipyards; insurer fortresses; silent data satellites; abandoned claims courts; a deep archive containing pre-Severance military movements.

**Present order and factions:** The Sable Underwriters govern through access contracts. Vault technicians preserve data but cannot read many sealed records. Crown agents seek to erase specific war claims. An internal faction believes the archives should become public evidence.

**Active scenario:** A vault cascade is destroying records in an apparently random pattern. In fact, the affected files trace Crown preparations before the Severance and Meridian financing of those preparations.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate and decrypt the oldest claim chains. The mother ship can physically isolate the archive from the compromised network.

**Primary gameplay:** Infiltration, data triage, stealth combat, forensic reconstruction, and bargaining over information that implicates multiple regions.

**Local rule:** Evidence can be copied, sealed, traded, or destroyed. Who receives a record may matter more than whether the player obtains it.

**Decision:** Publish the archive; preserve it under neutral custody; trade selected records for alliances; or destroy the most destabilizing evidence.

**Persistent consequences:** Builds the Severance evidence set, changes Crown and Meridian hostility, and affects the legitimacy of later trials or settlements.

**Route ties:** Nacre’s concealed harms appear in the vault. Crown Prime is directly connected. Vesper provides covert routes for people trying to escape with records.

## Vesper — Vespara

**Identity:** A canyon freeport where every faction’s unofficial business becomes visible.

**Backstory:** Vespara’s deep canyon networks shielded settlements from orbital surveillance. During the Severance wars, deserters, smugglers, refugees, and intelligence services all used the same routes. The system survived by refusing to distinguish cleanly between commerce and crime.

**System structure:** Vespara; two moons; canyon cities; hidden landing shelves; surface caravan routes; orbital freeport; water-extraction towers; abandoned military tunnels; the cross-region passage to Seraph.

**Present order and factions:** Canyon guilds govern individual settlements. Smuggler captains control movement. Meridian houses maintain deniable warehouses. Seraph clergy and Crown intelligence use the system to move forbidden people and artifacts.

**Active scenario:** A refugee convoy carrying Sable witnesses is trapped after a guild sells its route to Crown agents. A second faction wants to use the refugees as leverage in the Seraph succession struggle.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can escort the convoy, but doing so openly may close Meridian ports. The Atlas Core can identify which passengers hold authentic evidence and which are planted agents.

**Primary gameplay:** Smuggling, pursuit, convoy defense, faction bargaining, and canyon navigation.

**Local rule:** Identity is transactional. Transponders, uniforms, and declared cargo change which actors attack, assist, or inspect the player.

**Decision:** Protect all refugees; extract only key witnesses; bargain with a canyon guild; or surrender the convoy to preserve access to the Seraph route.

**Persistent consequences:** Controls witness survival, smuggler support, Crown awareness, and access to covert travel or false identities.

**Route ties:** Sable generates the evidence. Kestrel competes for courier traffic. Seraph turns the convoy into a question of legitimacy and sanctuary.

## Kestrel — Kestrel

**Identity:** A mountain courier system fighting to remain independent of the networks it connects.

**Backstory:** Kestrel’s thin atmosphere and extreme terrain produced exceptional pilots. Its courier guilds maintained communication when official routes failed, making them indispensable to Pax diplomacy and Meridian trade. Exchange Houses now seek to acquire the guilds through debt and safety regulation.

**System structure:** Kestrel; one moon; mountain launch cities; cliffside hangars; atmospheric racing corridors; courier monasteries; avalanche-prone relay towers; a concealed free-beacon network; gateways to Pax and Meridian Prime.

**Present order and factions:** Independent courier guilds elect a Flight Council. Meridian creditors control fuel contracts. Pax intelligence recruits couriers. Young pilots favor open resistance while older captains fear economic isolation.

**Active scenario:** A series of “accidents” is killing pilots who refuse acquisition. The Flight Council is divided over whether to reveal its illegal free-beacon network, which could break Meridian control but invite military seizure.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can prove the accidents are targeted sabotage and can carry beacon components through blockaded airspace. The player also needs reliable couriers to coordinate later regional choices.

**Primary gameplay:** High-risk flight, investigation, escort, and political organization among fiercely independent crews.

**Local rule:** Route knowledge is earned through trust. Navigation shortcuts, hidden arrivals, and advance warnings depend on guild relationships rather than a universal map unlock.

**Decision:** Defend full independence; negotiate regulated autonomy; expose the free-beacon network to the public; or let Pax or Meridian assume protective control.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks courier intelligence, route shortcuts, evasion options, and changes how rapidly consequences propagate between regions.

**Route ties:** Pax provides diplomacy and covert demand. Meridian Prime provides capital and pressure. Vesper offers illicit alternatives that can either strengthen or corrupt the guilds.
