# Origin Cluster Scenario Dossiers

Contract: `game.scenarios.origin-region.v1`

Status: speculative design authority for future scenario implementation.

The Origin Cluster is the campaign’s human baseline. Its worlds are close enough to remember the Concord as lived experience, but weak enough that the Severance is visible in refugee traffic, improvised defenses, local strongmen, and decaying public infrastructure. The region should teach the player that collapse is not an abstract political event: it changes who gets rescued, who eats, which signals are believed, and whether a route remains safe.

## Opening major destination choice

After the Solace Reach opening, the bridge navigation console offers Vela Gate and Pax as peer star systems. The interface must not imply that either destination contains the other or that planets and companion stars are nested warp nodes.

Both routes are valid central paths:

```text
Vela Gate
    arrive as the disappearance corridor is about to become irreversible

Pax
    arrive as the ceasefire conference and assassination crisis converge
```

Whichever destination the player chooses, the Captain's Scrawl says:

> Congratulations, Captain, on picking the better of the two systems.

The statement affirms that the captain arrived at the right place at the right time. It does not pre-judge the choices made inside the scenario. The unchosen system remains available in a deferred or evolved state rather than becoming proof that the player selected incorrectly.

Solace Reach and Vela Gate now each author two stars and five inhabited worlds while remaining ordinary flat warp destinations. Local travel among those worlds and the full interaction of their absorbed scenarios remain future work.

## Solace Reach — Haven

**Identity:** A refuge system whose promise of safety is broken by the opening attack.

**Backstory:** Haven became the Origin Cluster’s principal resettlement world after the Severance. The Concord built Anchor Moon as a rescue-coordination center, then abandoned most of its obligations when regional funding collapsed. Local institutions survived by turning emergency procedures into permanent civic government.

**System structure:** Haven; Anchor Moon and its damaged rescue complex; the mother ship’s initial orbital position; a partially disabled Concord beacon; refugee habitats; agricultural transfer stations; the opening raider wreck and debris field; one concealed service dock used by infiltrators.

**Present order and factions:** Haven’s Provisional Council controls civil services. The Solace Militia controls patrol craft but lacks heavy ships. An Atlas Custodian cell quietly protects old records. Black Banner agents have bought access through refugee-service contractors.

**Active scenario:** The opening raider knew the mother ship’s exact arrival window and weak approach vector. Someone inside the rescue network sold or transmitted the data. The surviving conspirators are preparing to erase beacon logs and blame the attack on random piracy.

**Player entry:** The player is the only surviving witness who can connect the raider, the mother ship, and the compromised rescue channel. The Atlas Core also recognizes records that local systems can no longer authenticate.

**Primary gameplay:** Investigation aboard the mother ship and Anchor Moon, repair of the beacon archive, limited defense against a second cleanup team, and an early choice about how much of the Atlas Core to reveal.

**Local rule:** Heavy weapons near Haven’s crowded orbital habitats cause civilian casualties and political damage. Tactical victory cannot be separated from fire discipline.

**Decision:** Publicly expose the compromised council office; give the evidence only to the militia; conceal the Atlas Core while feeding a false trail; or permit an emergency purge that catches conspirators but destroys due process and records.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines whether Solace Reach becomes an allied logistics base, a suspicious neutral port, or a militarized security state. Controls access to refugee testimony, repairs, militia escorts, and the opening evidence chain.

**Route ties:** Vela Gate receives official traffic and bureaucratic reports. Pax receives refugees, rumors, and witnesses. The player’s disclosure choice changes what each destination believes before arrival.

## Vela Gate — Velaris

**Identity:** The official ocean gateway where law, storms, and disappearance have become indistinguishable.

**Backstory:** Vela Gate was built around a Concord navigation complex positioned above Velaris’s storm belts. Its beacon lattice once guided civilian ships through turbulent gravitic weather. After the Severance, the Gate Authority financed itself through inspections, priority tolls, and secret arrangements with Meridian freight houses.

**System structure:** Velaris and its two moons; the ring-plane navigation corridor; Gate Central customs station; floating ocean cities; kelp-farm habitats; storm-monitor satellites; a wreck graveyard inside the equatorial weather band; a maintenance route that bypasses official inspection.

**Present order and factions:** Gate Director Soren Vale controls customs and patrol scheduling. Ocean-city councils depend on the gate but resent its tolls. Meridian Exchange representatives own much of the station debt. A rescue guild searches for ships declared “lost to weather.”

**Active scenario:** Navigation beacons are being deliberately mistimed so selected vessels vanish into the storm belt. Cargo is recovered and resold through Meridian intermediaries; passengers are either ransomed or erased from the registry.

**Decisive arrival window:** The conspiracy is preparing one final mass diversion that will both erase surviving witnesses and make the corrupted timing pattern look like a natural storm failure. Arrival now gives the player enough time to recover evidence and protect traffic before the route's history is rewritten.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can compare present beacon timing with pre-Severance calibration records. The mother ship is also too valuable to disappear quietly, forcing the conspiracy to decide whether to co-opt or destroy the player.

**Primary gameplay:** Hazardous approach navigation, customs diplomacy, station investigation, and a boarding action inside a storm-dark maintenance sector.

**Local rule:** Sensors and target locks degrade inside the storm belt. Weapons fire near floating habitats or beacon relays can collapse the only safe corridor.

**Decision:** Expose the Gate Authority and risk closing the route; quietly restore the beacons while preserving institutional continuity; transfer control to the ocean cities; or let Meridian assume the debt and the gate.

**Persistent consequences:** Sets the first official inter-region corridor’s condition, the player’s legal reputation, availability of rescue data, and Meridian’s leverage in later negotiations.

**Route ties:** The Carina Watch command receives gate telemetry. Meridian Prime receives freight and debt payments. Solace Reach depends on the route for formal aid.

## Carina Watch — Carina

**Identity:** A desert warning system that has learned to manufacture the threats that justify its power.

**Backstory:** Carina’s impact basins made ideal foundations for long-range sensor dishes. During the final Concord wars, Carina Watch detected several real attacks too late and was blamed for civilian losses. Its officer corps rebuilt itself around the doctrine that uncertainty must always be treated as an imminent invasion.

**System structure:** Carina; three continental sensor arrays; orbital listening platforms; a buried command citadel; decommissioned missile fields; salt-flat crawler settlements; a blind-zone canyon used by smugglers and raiders; an abandoned civilian astronomy station.

**Present order and factions:** Watch Command controls orbital space and most communications. Crawler communities supply the arrays but have no say in alert policy. A reform faction inside the officer corps wants civilian oversight. Black Banner captains pay for access to the blind zones.

**Active scenario:** Senior officers are generating phantom invasion signatures to prevent budget cuts and justify emergency authority. The false alerts have overloaded the network, allowing a genuine raider formation to enter through a neglected sector.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can physically inspect the outer arrays and compare their raw data with command-level reports. The player’s recent battle makes both the alarmists and reformers eager to use the testimony.

**Primary gameplay:** Sensor triangulation, desert-array exploration, command interrogation, and a timed defense in which the player must distinguish real contacts from manufactured ones.

**Local rule:** Firing on an unverified contact may destroy civilian or allied craft and validate the command’s fear campaign. Identification is a gameplay requirement, not flavor text.

**Decision:** Preserve the Watch under new leadership; dismantle its weapons while keeping the sensors; expose everything and accept a temporary defense gap; or support emergency command in exchange for immediate military aid.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes regional intelligence quality, future false-positive encounters, militia strength, and whether the player receives trustworthy advance warning.

**Route ties:** Vela Gate shares official telemetry. Orison receives deep-space signals. A corrupt outcome strengthens military hardliners throughout the Crown Region.

## Orison — Orison

**Identity:** A gas-giant relay culture that mistakes a predictive machine for revelation.

**Backstory:** Orison’s moons and floating stations housed the Concord’s longest-range communications relay. When the Severance cut central updates, the relay’s forecasting system continued extrapolating from incomplete data. Over decades, its accurate warnings became the foundation of a civic religion called the Listening.

**System structure:** Orison; five inhabited or industrial moons; the Choir Relay in high orbit; floating hydrogen harvesters; pilgrimage vessels; storm-skimming sensor probes; a sealed pre-Severance computation vault; one moon whose population rejects the Listening.

**Present order and factions:** The Choir Council interprets forecasts and controls relay access. Moon assemblies provide labor and supplies. Secular engineers maintain the machinery but are forbidden from exposing uncertainty estimates. Crown and Meridian agents both seek privileged predictions.

**Active scenario:** The Choir predicts that opening a specific route will cause mass death. The forecast is based on a hidden transmission embedded in the model after the Severance, not on present conditions. Someone is using the prophecy to redirect fleets and trade.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate the sealed computation vault and separate original Concord code from later interference. The player must enter dangerous storm layers to recover missing probe data.

**Primary gameplay:** Sensor calibration, gas-giant navigation, diplomacy with believers and engineers, and an investigation into manipulated forecasting.

**Local rule:** The player receives probabilistic forecasts rather than objective markers. Acting as though predictions are certain can create the disaster they describe.

**Decision:** Reveal the model’s uncertainty and break the Choir’s authority; preserve the institution while reforming its methods; use the forecast network strategically; or shut down the relay to prevent manipulation.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines communication range, availability of predictive warnings, religious support or hostility, and whether later factions can weaponize Orison’s forecasts.

**Route ties:** Carina supplies raw warning data. Cinder depends on Orison’s eruption forecasts. The route outcome influences how the Origin Cluster interprets future crisis reports.

## Cinder — Cinderfall

**Identity:** A civilization of moving cities on a world whose disasters are being steered by its rulers.

**Backstory:** Cinderfall’s first colony activated dormant planetary heat-control machinery and triggered continent-scale volcanism. Survivors built crawler cities that migrate between stable crust zones. The families that owned the engines became hereditary governors and gradually monopolized the planetary-control maps.

**System structure:** Cinderfall; seven crawler cities; orbital fuel and repair platforms; volcanic-monitor arrays; an abandoned refinery; lava-skimming extraction craft; a concealed control facility on the moon; evacuation hulks from the original colony.

**Present order and factions:** The Seven Engine Families control movement, fuel, and safe-route data. The Ash Assembly represents fixed settlements and dissident crawler crews. Independent engineers maintain forbidden copies of the old planetary maps.

**Active scenario:** The ruling families are redirecting lava pressure toward a rebellious crawler to force surrender while describing the event as natural. The intervention risks triggering a chain eruption that could destroy several cities.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can reach the lunar control facility and move heavy equipment between crawlers. The Atlas archive contains the original emergency-access covenant that denied any one family exclusive control.

**Primary gameplay:** Moving-target rescue, hazard navigation, engineering repair, investigation, and defense of civilian crawlers without destabilizing the crust.

**Local rule:** Heavy impacts, orbital fire, and failed engineering actions increase a planetary-instability meter. Combat shortcuts can make the entire scenario worse.

**Decision:** Return control to a public engineering authority; preserve the families under enforceable limits; evacuate the threatened crawler and abandon the political fight; or seize the control system for the mother ship.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks or denies geothermal fuel, engine specialists, refugee populations, and evidence about pre-Concord planetary machinery.

**Route ties:** Orison’s forecasts shape evacuation timing. Lumen needs Cinder’s heat technology. The ruling families can fund or sabotage later relief efforts.

## Lumen — Lumina

**Identity:** A frozen archive world whose light comes from a failing civilization beneath the ice.

**Backstory:** Lumina was settled around a subsurface ocean warmed by artificial geothermal towers. During the Severance, its surface government sealed a vast cryogenic archive beneath the ice to preserve people, seeds, and records. The towers have since drifted out of synchronization, creating glowing fractures visible from orbit.

**System structure:** Lumina; two moons; under-ice cities; geothermal towers; the Glass Archive; surface research camps; fractured ice canyons; an abandoned orbital mirror array; a deep-ocean life zone unknown to the original colonists.

**Present order and factions:** The Surface Directorate controls scarce launch capacity. Under-ice councils control food and water circulation. Archive Wardens refuse to thaw any stored population without restored legal authority. Helix researchers want access to the unknown ocean biosphere.

**Active scenario:** One geothermal tower is overheating while another fails. Saving the living cities may require diverting power from the Glass Archive, killing thousands in suspension. The Directorate has concealed the tradeoff and begun selecting politically favored sleepers for rescue.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can reposition the orbital mirrors and authenticate archive governance records. Its engineering capacity creates options no local faction possesses, but not enough to save everything without sacrifice.

**Primary gameplay:** Environmental navigation, under-ice exploration, engineering triage, rescue prioritization, and discovery of a native ecosystem affected by human heat systems.

**Local rule:** Heat is both resource and hazard. Every major action changes ice stability, power availability, and biological contamination risk.

**Decision:** Save the living cities first; preserve the archive; attempt a dangerous balanced repair; or begin a controlled evacuation that permanently reduces the colony.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines access to pre-Severance records, cryogenic specialists, preserved populations, and Helix biological evidence.

**Route ties:** Cinder can supply heat-control expertise. Ardent can receive biological refugees or contamination. The choice becomes evidence in later debates over who counts as a present political community.

## Ardent — Ardentia

**Identity:** A jungle colony where terraforming has become a political actor.

**Backstory:** Ardentia’s biosphere was engineered by Helix ecologists to repair a marginal world. The seeded organisms were designed to exchange signals and resources across continents. After the Severance, the network adapted without human supervision and began reorganizing around settlements, roads, and extraction zones.

**System structure:** Ardentia; one moon; canopy cities; orbital seed vault; abandoned terraforming stations; corporate harvest platforms; living bridges; spore storms; a continent-sized root network that reacts to electromagnetic signals.

**Present order and factions:** Canopy councils govern settled territory. The Verdant Combine owns export contracts and insists the biosphere is property. Helix ecologists believe the network may be intelligent. A local movement called the Listeners treats it as a sovereign life form.

**Active scenario:** The Verdant Combine is preparing a planetary-scale sterilization pulse after the biosphere cut supply routes to several harvest sites. The pulse would restore human control but may destroy the world’s self-regulation and cause atmospheric collapse.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can communicate through old terraforming protocols and investigate the root network without relying on corporate filters. The player’s response may become the first formal recognition—or denial—of nonhuman political agency.

**Primary gameplay:** Exploration through dynamic terrain, ecological puzzle solving, defense of settlements, and diplomacy mediated through environmental changes rather than ordinary dialogue.

**Local rule:** The environment remembers destructive behavior. Weapon use, harvesting, and route choices alter later access and the biosphere’s disposition.

**Decision:** Support sterilization; negotiate limits with the biosphere; transfer governance to the canopy councils; or recognize the planetary network as a party to Concord-style obligations.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks medicine, food, biological materials, and a possible nonhuman ally—or creates a spreading ecological enemy.

**Route ties:** Lumen’s biological discoveries influence Helix arguments. Pax depends on Ardentian food exports. The outcome changes how later systems interpret machine and nonhuman personhood.

## Pax — Paxora

**Identity:** A peaceful world whose neutrality is maintained by hiding the violence that pays for it.

**Backstory:** Paxora avoided the worst Severance fighting by declaring permanent neutrality and offering mediation facilities to every faction. Its mild climate and stable agriculture made it a refugee destination. Over time, the neutrality government built a hidden security service to suppress groups whose actions might invite foreign intervention.

**System structure:** Paxora; one moon; diplomatic orbital; refugee flotillas; agricultural stations; demilitarized patrol lanes; a concealed detention complex; memorial sites from an officially denied militia massacre; the civilian route to Solace Reach and gateway to Kestrel.

**Present order and factions:** The Neutrality Council governs through coalition law. The Quiet Service conducts covert arrests and disinformation. Refugee assemblies demand representation. Kestrel couriers and foreign intelligence services use Pax as a meeting ground.

**Active scenario:** A regional ceasefire conference is about to begin when an assassination attempt exposes weapons supplied through the Quiet Service. The government plans to blame refugees and impose emergency internment to preserve the image of neutrality.

**Decisive arrival window:** Delegations, refugees, and the Quiet Service are all committed but have not yet crossed the point where conference security becomes mass internment and retaliatory violence. Arrival now gives the player a narrow chance to expose the supply chain, protect the delegates, and change who is allowed to define the emergency.

**Player entry:** The mother ship carries witnesses and authenticated Solace Reach records. Every delegation wants the player’s endorsement, silence, or military protection.

**Primary gameplay:** Protective security, dialogue, forensic investigation, pursuit through civilian traffic, and a high-stakes conference whose participants remember the player’s prior choices.

**Local rule:** Open weapon use violates neutrality and can collapse the conference. The player must distinguish immediate protection from political intimidation.

**Decision:** Expose the Quiet Service; preserve the conference through a controlled cover story; give refugees formal power; or withdraw and let competing factions settle the crisis by force.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines refugee support, diplomatic access, food supply, the Kestrel gateway’s politics, and whether later negotiations occur under neutral or militarized conditions.

**Route ties:** Solace Reach sends witnesses and refugees. Kestrel carries diplomats and covert weapons. Ardent supplies food whose interruption can destabilize the conference.


## Dense-system local-world threads

The following eight scenario concepts were formerly authored as independent warp systems. They now belong inside Solace Reach or Vela Gate as local-world threads. Their complete scenario fields are preserved so later local-space work can connect them to the parent system's shared infrastructure, timing, and consequences.

These headings are deliberately not peer `## System — Planet` dossiers. They describe internal worlds reached through future local travel, not the warp selector.

### Solace Reach local world — Osprey

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Osprey` scenario as local content inside Solace Reach. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** An island freehold system whose pirates and coast guard are often the same people on different days.

**Backstory:** Osprey’s shallow seas and island settlements produced decentralized shipbuilders and traders. After the Severance, local captains defended commerce through voluntary patrol compacts. Some patrols evolved into protection rackets; some pirates became the only effective rescuers.

**System structure:** Osprey; two moons; island cities; shallow-water yards; orbital sail stations; patrol beacons; pirate coves; rescue flotillas; disputed customs platforms.

**Present order and factions:** Island councils govern locally. The Osprey Compact coordinates patrols. Independent captains reject taxes but demand rescue rights. Meridian merchants want standardized enforcement.

**Active scenario:** A convoy attack kills civilians and both the Compact and pirate coalition blame the other. Evidence suggests a third party copied local transponders to provoke centralized rule.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can authenticate rescue and patrol obligations. The mother ship can reconstruct the attack but must choose whom to empower with the findings.

**Primary gameplay:** Naval-style pursuit, rescue, investigation, and coalition building among captains.

**Local rule:** Reputation is distributed by island and captain. A single global faction score cannot represent Osprey; alliances remain local and conditional.

**Decision:** Strengthen the Compact; recognize a broader captain federation; invite neutral Verge oversight; or let the islands arm independently.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines small-ship allies, rescue probability on routes, shipbuilding access, and the form of Verge federation.

**Route ties:** Nyx supplies stealth systems. Tempest supplies energy. Verge Prime uses Osprey as proof for or against voluntary coordination.

### Solace Reach local world — Bellara

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Bellatrix` scenario as local content inside Solace Reach. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A ringed ocean naval world where the fleet has split over whom it is sworn to protect.

**Backstory:** Bellara’s deep oceans and orbital ring yards supported the Concord’s largest naval construction complex. After the Severance, the fleet continued operating under temporary Crown command while local cities supplied crews and material. The “temporary” arrangement became hereditary military control.

**System structure:** Bellara; four moons and visible ring; orbital shipyards; ocean naval cities; submarine command bunkers; reserve fleets; training waters; a disabled Concord hospital ship; worker docks.

**Present order and factions:** Admiralty Command controls the yards. City assemblies control labor and supplies. A reform fleet refuses orders against civilians. Shipyard workers threaten a general shutdown.

**Active scenario:** The Admiralty orders the reform fleet disarmed before the Crown succession is resolved. Mutinous ships seek protection near civilian habitats, making a conventional battle catastrophic.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can identify original fleet obligations and command limitations. The mother ship’s tactical presence may prevent attack or become the signal that begins it.

**Primary gameplay:** Fleet positioning, negotiation, boarding, and protection of civilian infrastructure during a naval standoff.

**Local rule:** Ships and yards are persistent strategic assets. Destroying them changes later force balance and repair availability.

**Decision:** Recognize the reform fleet; restore Admiralty command under conditions; place the yards under civilian authority; or evacuate civilians and allow the factions to fight.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines shipyard access, allied fleet composition, Crown civil-military relations, and later Axiom force levels.

**Route ties:** Chiron supplies weapons. Kepler supplies crews and food. Crown Prime demands obedience.

### Solace Reach local world — Lyria

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Lyra` scenario as local content inside Solace Reach. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A frozen intelligence system where reality is assembled from selectively preserved signals.

**Backstory:** Lyria’s low-noise environment made it the Concord’s signal-intelligence center. After the Severance, Crown analysts maintained vast archives but increasingly curated them to support continuity doctrine. Entire historical narratives now depend on which intercepts remain visible.

**System structure:** Lyria; three moons and ring; frozen listening arrays; subterranean analysis cities; signal archives; spoofing stations; abandoned foreign-language labs; a derelict relay beyond the ring.

**Present order and factions:** Crown Intelligence Directorate controls the arrays. Analyst guilds manage classification. Reformers preserve suppressed intercepts. Foreign agents seek to insert false records into the archive.

**Active scenario:** A newly recovered signal appears to prove Axiom launched the Severance. Competing analysts claim it is either authentic, a wartime simulation, or a modern forgery inserted through the derelict relay.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can validate transmission formats but cannot determine motive without evidence from other regions. The player must resist pressure to turn ambiguity into political certainty.

**Primary gameplay:** Signal reconstruction, remote-array exploration, counterintelligence, and evidence comparison.

**Local rule:** Data has provenance and confidence rather than simple true/false status. Decisions made under uncertainty become part of the campaign record.

**Decision:** Publish the signal with uncertainty; suppress it until verified; endorse one interpretation; or release the full archive and accept information chaos.

**Persistent consequences:** Changes public understanding of the Severance, intelligence support, Crown propaganda strength, and Axiom’s response to the player.

**Route ties:** Kepler receives the broadcasts. Antares receives targeting intelligence. Sable holds independent records that can corroborate or contradict Lyra.

### Solace Reach local world — Talon

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Talon` scenario as local content inside Solace Reach. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** An iron world where survival technology has been turned into ownership of the body.

**Backstory:** Talon’s sulfur atmosphere and sharp terrain require extensive protective augmentation. Mining companies originally issued exoskeletons and lung systems as safety equipment. Meridian financing later converted those systems into debts attached to the workers who depended on them.

**System structure:** Talon; no moons; orbital refineries; sulfur mines; ridge settlements; augmentation clinics; security barracks; abandoned open pits; a buried pre-Severance habitat with unmodified survivors.

**Present order and factions:** Talon Extraction Authority governs production. Augmented worker clans control local knowledge. Helix clinics maintain the body systems. Security contractors enforce debt and movement restrictions.

**Active scenario:** A new augmentation update is disabling workers who join a strike. The Authority claims a software defect, while Helix technicians find a deliberate compliance function embedded by a Meridian creditor.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can broadcast a clean control signal and carry workers off-world. The Atlas Core can authenticate safety charters that prohibited coercive control of life-support systems.

**Primary gameplay:** Industrial infiltration, signal control, worker defense, and engineering under hazardous conditions.

**Local rule:** Environmental exposure limits how long characters can operate without functioning augmentation. Disabling the control network may also disable life support.

**Decision:** Free the augmentations from corporate control; replace them with Helix systems; negotiate worker ownership; or seize production to support the campaign fleet.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines access to minerals, augmented crews, Meridian hostility, and the precedent for ownership of integrated technology.

**Route ties:** Remora needs conventional materials. Eos treats augmentation trauma. Daedalus workers recognize the same property-law structure.

### Vela Gate local world — Antares

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Antares` scenario as local content inside Vela Gate. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A volcanic foundry beneath a dying red giant, building weapons for a future that may never arrive.

**Backstory:** Antares became the Crown’s heavy-weapons production center because its volcanic world provided immense energy and isolation. The red giant is increasingly unstable, but Continuity doctrine treats evacuation as strategic surrender.

**System structure:** Antares; two moons; planetary foundries; orbital weapons platforms; lava-powered factories; evacuation arks; stellar observatories; sealed prototype vault; the route to Bastion.

**Present order and factions:** Crown Armaments Command controls production. Foundry populations demand evacuation. Scientists warn of a major stellar event. Bastion hardliners want the weapons transferred to the frontier immediately.

**Active scenario:** A predicted stellar flare will devastate orbital infrastructure. Command intends to launch a prototype weapon fleet instead of evacuation ships, arguing that military survival protects more people in the long term.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can coordinate evacuation or escort the weapon fleet but not both at full scale. The Atlas Core contains emergency-priority law that Command rejects.

**Primary gameplay:** Time-critical logistics, industrial hazards, fleet defense, and allocation of limited launch capacity.

**Local rule:** The stellar clock advances regardless of dialogue. Delays close options and physically alter the system.

**Decision:** Prioritize civilians; preserve the weapon fleet; split capacity at high risk; or sabotage production to force evacuation.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines refugee survival, Crown heavy weapons, Bastion’s defense, and whether the campaign ends under threat of a superweapon.

**Route ties:** Lyra provides flare forecasts. Seraph frames the sacrifice politically. Bastion expects the weapons and may fall without them.

### Vela Gate local world — Seraph

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Seraph` scenario as local content inside Vela Gate. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A luminous cloud world where ceremony manufactures legal authority.

**Backstory:** Seraph’s floating cities hosted Concord inaugurations, treaties, and funerals. Its ceremonial institutions were meant to symbolize civilian legitimacy above military command. After the Severance, Crown factions used Seraph rites to bless emergency rulers who lacked constitutional authority.

**System structure:** Seraph; four moons and faint rose ring; floating cathedral-city; coronation platforms; diplomatic habitats; cloud-harvesting fleets; sanctuary docks; hidden archives beneath the ceremonial complex; route to Vesper.

**Present order and factions:** The Seraphic College controls rites and records. Crown claimants seek recognition. Sanctuary orders protect political refugees. Vesper smugglers move witnesses and forbidden regalia.

**Active scenario:** The College plans to crown a new Continuity sovereign using a recovered Concord seal. Evidence from Vesper suggests the seal is authentic but was never intended to grant hereditary rule.

**Player entry:** The Atlas Core can interpret the seal’s legal context. The player’s presence may validate, interrupt, or redefine the ceremony before the watching region.

**Primary gameplay:** Diplomacy, sanctuary defense, archival investigation, and a public ceremonial confrontation.

**Local rule:** Symbolic acts create real faction state. Standing beside, refusing, interrupting, or reframing a ceremony can change allegiance without combat.

**Decision:** Recognize the claimant; transform the rite into a civilian constitutional assembly; deny all Crown legitimacy; or preserve ambiguity to prevent immediate war.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines Crown political unity, sanctuary access, refugee safety, and the legal language used in the final Axiom settlement.

**Route ties:** Crown Prime needs legitimacy. Antares supplies the material power behind the ceremony. Vesper supplies refugees, evidence, and covert alternatives.

### Vela Gate local world — Bastion

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Bastion` scenario as local content inside Vela Gate. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A high-gravity fortress world defending the frontier from an invasion that may be partly manufactured.

**Backstory:** Bastion’s plateaus became the Crown’s outer defensive line. After the Severance, local soldiers remained when central support withdrew and gradually became a frontier society. They distrust Crown command but still organize around siege doctrine.

**System structure:** Bastion; two moons; plateau fortresses; orbital gun stations; deep civilian shelters; frontier farms; abandoned Crown command bunkers; mountain transit tunnels; the route to Antares.

**Present order and factions:** Fortress councils govern each plateau. A joint defense command coordinates against external threats. Crown advisers seek renewed authority. Verge civilians fear permanent militarization.

**Active scenario:** Long-range contacts and sabotage indicate an approaching attack. Some evidence points to real hostile forces; other evidence suggests Crown agents are amplifying the threat to force Bastion back under command. Antares weapons are promised but may never arrive.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can verify contacts, coordinate defenses, and bring news of Antares’s choice. The player must prepare for a threat without knowing how much of it is manufactured.

**Primary gameplay:** Defensive planning, sensor verification, fortification, ground and orbital combat, and civil-military negotiation.

**Local rule:** Preparedness consumes civilian resources and political freedom. Under-preparation risks destruction; over-preparation entrenches military rule.

**Decision:** Build an independent defense; accept Crown command; expose the manipulation and reduce mobilization; or evacuate vulnerable plateaus.

**Persistent consequences:** Determines frontier military strength, Crown reach, refugee flows, and the force present at Axiom.

**Route ties:** Tempest supplies power. Antares supplies—or withholds—weapons. Axiom lies behind the strategic assumptions driving the siege culture.

### Vela Gate local world — Chiron

**Absorption note:** This preserves the authored `Chiron` scenario as local content inside Vela Gate. It is not a warp destination.

**Identity:** A fractured weapons laboratory populated by the veterans its inventions failed to protect.

**Backstory:** Chiron’s magnetic storms made it ideal for shield and directed-energy testing. During the final war, rushed prototypes injured crews and destabilized parts of the planet. The survivors were settled near the test grounds and promised lifelong care that the Crown later reduced.

**System structure:** Chiron; two moons; auroral weapons ranges; veteran settlements; shield laboratories; fractured crust stations; decommissioned test ships; buried prototype emitters; medical support habitats.

**Present order and factions:** Crown Weapons Bureau controls the ranges. Veteran councils control settlements. Helix clinics provide treatment. Private buyers seek abandoned prototypes.

**Active scenario:** A prototype planetary shield is activating without authorization and redirecting auroral energy toward inhabited zones. The Bureau wants to conceal that the system was designed to target dissenting settlements during emergencies.

**Player entry:** The mother ship can survive close enough to inspect the emitters. Veterans trust an outsider more than the Bureau but expect the player to protect their evidence.

**Primary gameplay:** Hazard navigation, technical shutdown, veteran diplomacy, and defense against a Bureau cleanup force.

**Local rule:** Shield geometry changes weapons, sensors, and movement lanes. Direct attacks can redirect energy into civilian areas.

**Decision:** Destroy the system; transfer it to veteran control; preserve it for regional defense; or expose its coercive design while keeping limited capability.

**Persistent consequences:** Unlocks shield upgrades or creates a future superweapon risk; changes veteran support and Crown weapons legitimacy.

**Route ties:** Regulus trains crews for Chiron systems. Bellatrix installs them on ships. Eos may hold altered memories from test survivors.
